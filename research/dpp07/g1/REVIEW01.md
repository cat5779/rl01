# Independent mathematical audit of `proof01.md`

STATUS: CRITICAL_GAPS

## Scope and verdict

I audited the frozen statement and all of Sections 1--5, with particular attention to the full-exterior conditional version, product continuity, the transport matrix, the finite-flow/weak-limit/Poisson-completion chain, the scalar entrance, the ungapped endpoint, and all-input totalization.

The algebraic FK path, the canonical conditional odds under a two-sided spectral gap, the transport row/column estimates, and the scalar-entrance asymptotics are consistent.  The manuscript nevertheless does **not** establish the claimed iid factor.  The decisive missing work is in Section 4: the passage from finite envelope processes to a weak process and then to an iid Poisson driver is asserted across several nontrivial probability-theoretic interfaces.  In particular, the text does not prove the joint no-common-jump statement needed for its Poisson characterization.  Consequently the later Picard strong solution, and hence the total Borel iid map, do not follow from the proof as written.

This verdict concerns completeness of the submitted proof.  It is not a counterexample to the frozen theorem.

## Critical gap 1: coordinate compensators do not establish a Poisson random measure

The load-bearing passage is `proof01.md`, lines 365--410, especially lines 371--386.

The preceding argument gives at most convergence of finite-coordinate compensator identities.  The text then marks true births, adds points above the intensity, and asserts that

\[
 \exp\!\left(-\int h\,dN+\int(1-e^{-h})\,d\nu\right)
\]

is a bounded martingale and therefore that (N) is an iid Poisson field in the common filtration.  No proof is given that distinct coordinates of the weak limit have no simultaneous jumps, equivalently that all cross-jump compensators vanish.  That property is not a consequence of knowing the individual coordinate intensities.

There is a simple obstruction to the inference being used.  Let (M) be a rate-one Poisson process and put (N_1=N_2=M).  In the filtration generated by (M), each coordinate has deterministic compensator (t), but the coordinates jump simultaneously and are not independent Poisson processes.  For a constant (h>0), the proposed two-coordinate exponential has expectation

\[
 \exp\{t(e^{-2h}-1)+2t(1-e^{-h})\}
 =\exp\{t(1-e^{-h})^2\}\ne1,
\]

so the claimed exponential-martingale conclusion fails precisely in the presence of common jumps.

Every finite envelope process is driven by sitewise Poisson proposals and has no simultaneous finite-site jumps, but weak convergence alone does not preserve that property without a uniform two-jump estimate or passage of the full finite-dimensional martingale problem.  The manuscript supplies neither.  A repair would have to prove, for every finite set of distinct sites, the single-jump generator martingale problem (or an (O(h^2)) estimate for two births in an interval of length (h)); applying it to products of occupation coordinates would then force zero cross variation.  Only after that step can a marking theorem and a Watanabe-type Poisson characterization be invoked.  The same missing joint conditional-Laplace argument is what is needed to conclude that (N) is independent of (W_0).

Because the Picard iteration in lines 398--410 is driven by this unproved iid Poisson field, the claimed strong solution and iid factor are not established.

## Critical gap 2: the finite conditional chain and weak-limit martingale problem are not actually passed to the limit

The second incomplete interface is `proof01.md`, lines 330--374.

For a finite (F), equation (21) does give the correct projected rate and the finite forward equation determines a finite pure-birth chain.  Equation (22) also gives the right pointwise sandwich while the local order is intact.  However, the manuscript then jumps from the boundary-chain estimate (23) to the exact total-variation estimate (24) without defining the three-process coupling on a growing finite set, retaining the boundary error, and taking the required limit in (F).  The missing intermediate statement should have the form

\[
 \|\mathcal L(L_s^n|_{B_0})-\nu_s|_{B_0}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le |B_0|p_n(S)+\varepsilon_{F,n,B_0}(S),
 \qquad \varepsilon_{F,n,B_0}(S)\to0
\]

for fixed (n,B_0,S) as the dependency distance from (B_0) to the boundary of (F) tends to infinity.  One must construct the coupling for which this inequality holds before deleting the boundary term.  The prose around (23) describes the intended argument but does not prove (24).

Similarly, (25) is an unconditional mean error bound for compensators.  It does not by itself justify the sentence “Passing finite-coordinate martingale identities to the limit.”  That passage needs a declared path topology, joint convergence with bounded functions of the past, convergence of the predictable integrals, and control of jumps at deterministic times.  Those steps are also needed for the no-simultaneous-jump conclusion in Critical gap 1.  Since exact marginals, adaptedness, and the later Poisson completion all depend on this passage, this is a load-bearing omission rather than a cosmetic compression.

## Checks that passed

### Sections 1--2: FK path and full-exterior conditional odds

For (0<p<1), differentiating (K_t=a_t(Q+tI)) gives (1), and Jacobi's formula gives (3).  The finite (L)-ensemble conditional odds are the Schur complement in (4); substituting them into the death equation and reversing time gives (5).

For bounded positive invertible (L), formula (7) is the shorted operator formula.  If (eta_n\to\eta) in the product topology, then (D_{eta_n}\to D_eta) strongly.  The operators

\[
 D_{eta_n}LD_{eta_n}+I-D_{eta_n}
\]

have a uniform positive lower bound, so their inverses converge strongly.  Thus (7) does yield a total Borel, covariant, product-continuous version.  With a two-sided spectral gap, finite compressed kernels can be extended off the finite set with a fixed gapped scalar; continuous functional calculus then gives strong convergence of their (L)-operators, and bounded martingale convergence identifies (8) with conditioning on the whole exterior.  The manuscript compresses this argument into line 143, but the claimed conclusion is valid under the stated gap.

Formula (9) is also correct: for (L_t=K_t(I-K_t)^{-1}),

\[
 \langle L_t^{-1}\delta_x,\delta_x\rangle=a_t^{-1}r_t-1,
\]

using regular-orbit equivariance.  Hence the minimum full-exterior conditional probability is (a_t/r_t), and the birth rate is nonnegative.

### Section 3: transport sensitivity

For (eta\subseteq\zeta), the polar part of

\[
 (I-\Pi_eta^L)L^{1/2}D_{zeta\setminus\eta}
\]

has final projection (Pi_zeta^L-Pi_eta^L), which gives (12).  The identity

\[
 (Pi_zeta^L-Pi_eta^L)L^{-1/2}(I-D_zeta)=0
\]

justifies insertion of (L-\ell I).  Parseval on the initial space of the polar isometry gives the row sum, while

\[
 \sum_{x\notin\zeta}w(x,y)
 =\|(I-D_zeta)(L-\ell I)L^{-1/2}V\delta_y\|^2
\]

gives the column bound (13).  Comparing both configurations with their intersection accounts for the factor (2), and the source-site diagonal term accounts for the case in which exactly one source is occupied.  Covariance plus the mass-transport identity then gives (16) for every jointly invariant pair.  No amenability assumption is needed for this step.

### Section 5: entrance and endpoint

The expansions in lines 432--448 are correct.  Since the diagonal of (Q-\tau(Q)I) is zero on a regular orbit, shorting (L_t=\ell I+uG+O(u^2)) subtracts only a quadratic off-diagonal term, uniformly in the occupied exterior.  Thus (26) holds uniformly.  The (u) and (u^2) terms cancel in

\[
 c_t=(r_t-a_t)\lambda_{L_t}-a_t,
\]

giving (overline c_t=O(u^3)); also ((r_t-a_t)\kappa(L_t)=O(u^3)).  After the time change, the rates and transport constant are (O(s)), so there is no entrance singularity.

Assuming the missing Section 4 construction, the ungapped endpoint argument is correct: monotonicity gives (Y=\bigcup_{s<1}Z_s), and finite inclusion probabilities converge to (det Q[B]), which identifies (Y\sim\mu_Q).  The cases (p=0) and (p=1) are also handled correctly under the stated interpretation of H; for (p=1), faithfulness of the trace forces (Q=I).

The final conull-set totalization is Borel and equivariant once a common strong graphical solution on all compact subintervals has actually been constructed.  It cannot repair the missing strong-solution step in Section 4.

## Quantifier conclusion

The manuscript does cover a fixed equivariant complex Hermitian contraction (Q), uses H only at (p=0), and does not claim a measurable grand coupling in (Q).  There is no detected weakening from all countable groups to amenable groups in Sections 1--3.  The failure is instead at the universal probabilistic construction: for arbitrary countable (Gamma), the submitted proof has not produced the required common iid input because its joint Poisson driver and strong solution are unproved.
