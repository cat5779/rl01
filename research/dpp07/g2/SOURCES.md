# Source scope and attribution of the pointwise FK counterexample

## Exact conclusion

The counterexample disproves the assertion that, for every equivariant positive contraction on a scalar regular orbit, the best ordinary Bernoulli domination parameter equals the Fuglede–Kadison determinant. It leaves the two FK domination inequalities intact. It does not by itself settle invariant coupling at the larger ordinary threshold.

## Original statement and strongest natural reading

Lyons's ICM survey, printed pp. 157–158, puts Conjecture 5.7 in a subsection fixing a sofic group, refers to the abelian Theorem 5.2 as the intended extension, and includes optimality. The kernel in the counterexample is on one regular orbit of the sofic group C3*C3; no passage to a nonsofic group or to a nonfree index action is responsible for the failure. [Published survey](https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/pdf/icm-pub.pdf)

The abelian antecedent has a pointwise necessary-and-sufficient meaning: Lyons–Steif Theorem 5.11 determines the domination parameters for every fixed measurable symbol. Definition 5.15 and Theorem 5.16 separately treat full-conditioning domination, whose threshold is the harmonic mean. Thus replacing ordinary domination with full-conditioning domination would change the problem. [Lyons–Steif](https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/pdf/dyn.pdf)

These sources support taking pointwise optimality seriously as a natural strong reading. They do not identify the author's unexpressed intent. A uniform sharpness assertion over all kernels is a distinct, weaker assertion: scalar kernels already attain the bounds. The mathematical statement should always be written explicitly, with this distinction visible.

The author-maintained [ICM errata dated 30 December 2025](https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/errata/icm.pdf) concerns Sections 3 and 4 and contains no correction of Conjecture 5.7. The [Lyons–Steif errata dated 29 November 2025](https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/errata/dyn.pdf) corrects Remark 5.12 and clarifies finite-set distance normalization, without changing Theorem 5.11. Absence from these two errata is only a fact about those documents, not an exhaustive literature-status assertion.

## Classical ingredients and contribution limits

1. **Tree domination:** BLPS, Section 11, proof of Theorem 11.1, couples independent parent-hitting percolation below the wired uniform spanning forest using Wilson's algorithm. On the regular tree its parameter is the reciprocal forward degree. This already supplies the domination ingredient used in the projection counterexample. The present Hilbert-space proof is an alternative derivation, not a claim to invent that domination phenomenon. [BLPS](https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/pdf/usf.pdf)
2. **Amenable determinant approximation:** Li–Thom Theorem 1.4 proves the finite-compression infimum and Følner limit formula for positive operators, including singular endpoints. Combined with a valid FK domination theorem, it gives pointwise equality for amenable groups. The determinant theorem itself is prior work. [Li–Thom](https://www.math.buffalo.edu/~hfli/entdettor17.pdf)
3. **Sofic membership:** the free product of finite groups is covered by closure under free products of sofic groups. [Elek–Szabó, Theorem 1](https://www.ams.org/proc/2011-139-12/S0002-9939-2011-11222-X/S0002-9939-2011-11222-X.pdf)

The remaining attribution question is whether the regular-orbit identification, its connection to pointwise optimality in Conjecture 5.7, or the nonsingular variants have already been stated. Targeted searches did not establish either priority or a published identical formulation. Novelty remains unconfirmed. The elementary derivation from classical ingredients must be disclosed when evaluating its contribution.

## Consequences for further work

The universal equality is retired. Constructive FK lower bounds, invariant ordered coupling, and sharp distance estimates are separate statements. The explicit tree example supplies a fixed test for the additional gap between ordinary domination, invariant monotone joining, and joint iid realization; none of the cited root-dependent couplings closes that gap automatically. The general connectivity/cost target remains open in this work.
