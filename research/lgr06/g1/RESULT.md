**INCOMPLETE.**

Neither the frozen target nor its negation is proved. The exact unclosed step for the assigned regular-iid FUSF route is

\[
\operatorname{relCost}(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{Ber}};\mathcal S_{\mathrm{FUSF}})=0.
\tag{B}
\]

Conditional on H77, (B) is exactly equivalent to repairing FUSF by arbitrary orbit edges of arbitrarily small expected degree. It implies not only

\[
\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)=1+\beta _1^{(2)}(\Gamma),
\]

but fixed price at that value. When \(\beta _1^{(2)}=0\), (B) is equivalent to fixed price one; this remains open for general property-\((T)\) groups. The cost/first-\(L^2\)-Betti equality itself is also still stated as open in current sources. 

## 1. Definitions and known input

For a pmp equivalence relation \(\mathcal R\) on \((X,\mu)\), a graphing is a countable family of measure-preserving partial isomorphisms
\(\phi_i:A_i\to B_i\) whose graphs lie in \(\mathcal R\). Its cost is
\(\sum_i\mu(A_i)\), and \(C(\mathcal R)\) is the infimum over graphings generating \(\mathcal R\). The target uses

\[
\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)
 =\inf_{\alpha\ \mathrm{essentially\ free\ pmp}}
 C(\mathcal R_\alpha),
\]

not Bernoulli cost and not fixed price.

**[KNOWN: Gaboriau]** For every essentially free pmp action of an infinite group,

\[
C(\mathcal R_\alpha)\ge 1+\beta _1^{(2)}(\Gamma).
\tag{1}
\]

For \(\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal R\), define

\[
\operatorname{relCost}(\mathcal R;\mathcal S)
 =\inf\{C(\Phi):\mathcal S\vee\Phi=\mathcal R\}.
\tag{2}
\]

If \(\mathcal S\) has infinite classes, then

\[
C(\mathcal R)-C(\mathcal S)
\le \operatorname{relCost}(\mathcal R;\mathcal S)
\le C(\mathcal R)-1.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus, when \(C(\mathcal S)=1\),

\[
\operatorname{relCost}(\mathcal R;\mathcal S)=C(\mathcal R)-1.
\tag{4}
\]

For a complete section \(A\), the induction formula is

\[
C(\mathcal R)-1
 =\mu(A)\bigl(C(\mathcal R|A,\mu_A)-1\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]

These are Proposition 2.33 and Proposition 2.39 in Gaboriau’s notes. 

**[KNOWN: Lyons and USF theory]** On every Cayley graph,

\[
\mathbb E\deg_{\mathrm{FUSF}}(o)
 =2+2\beta _1^{(2)}(\Gamma).
\tag{6}
\]

FUSF is a determinantal spanning forest, its components are infinite, and its edge graphing is a treeing. Lyons’s Theorem 5.10 records the original low-degree dominating-DPP route and explicitly identifies connectivity as the missing step. 

**[KNOWN: Abért–Weiss]** For finitely generated \(\Gamma\), Bernoulli actions have maximal cost among free pmp actions. Therefore, if Bernoulli cost reaches the lower bound in (1), every free action has that cost. Bernoulli cost one is equivalent to fixed price one. 

## 2. Signed orientations and edge stabilizers

**[PROVED_HERE, CONDITIONAL ON H77] Lemma 2.1.** FUSF on any finite-generator Cayley multigraph is a factor of regular iid \([0,1]^\Gamma\), including involutive generators.

**Proof.** Let \(E\) be the unoriented edge set and choose one orientation for each edge. Translation acts on \(\ell^2(E)\) by signed permutations

\[
U_g\delta_e=\sigma(g,e)\delta_{ge},
\qquad \sigma(g,e)\in\{\pm1\}.
\]

The transfer-current FUSF kernel \(Q\) is a positive contraction commuting with these signed unitaries.

Put \(\widetilde E=E\times\{\pm1\}\), with the honest permutation action

\[
g(e,\epsilon)=(ge,\sigma(g,e)\epsilon),
\]

and let

\[
J\delta_e
 =2^{-1/2}\bigl(\delta_{(e,+)}-\delta_{(e,-)}\bigr),
\qquad
\widetilde Q=JQJ^*.
\]

Then \(\widetilde Q\) is an equivariant positive contraction, so H77 gives its DPP \(\widetilde X\).

The determinant for \((e,+),(e,-)\) is zero, hence almost surely at most one point is selected in each fibre. Project to

\[
X=\{e:(e,+)\in\widetilde X\text{ or }(e,-)\in\widetilde X\}.
\]

For finite \(B\subset E\) and a sign choice
\(\epsilon:B\to\{\pm1\}\),

\[
\det\widetilde Q[(e,\epsilon_e)]_{e\in B}
 =2^{-|B|}\det Q[B].
\]

The \(2^{|B|}\) sign events are disjoint, so

\[
\mathbb P[B\subseteq X]=\det Q[B].
\]

The action on \(\widetilde E\) is free: a nonidentity translation stabilizing an unoriented Cayley edge must interchange its endpoints, in which case \(\sigma=-1\), so it fixes no signed lift. There are finitely many free orbits. Splitting one uniform label at each \(\gamma\) into finitely many independent uniforms identifies their noise with regular \(\Gamma\)-noise. ∎

## 3. Exact implication of a repair

Let \(\mathcal R\) be the orbit relation of the resulting Bernoulli action, let \(F\) be the FUSF factor, and let \(\mathcal S_F\) be the relation generated by FUSF edges.

**[PROVED_HERE, CONDITIONAL ON H77] Lemma 3.1.**

\[
C(\mathcal S_F)=1+\beta _1^{(2)}(\Gamma).
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** Orient FUSF edges by fresh iid continuous vertex labels. The resulting graphing is a treeing, so its cost is half its expected root degree. Apply (6). ∎

**[PROVED_HERE] Theorem 3.2.** Suppose that for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is an equivariant measurable orbit-edge process \(R\), using additional iid randomness, with

\[
F\cup R\text{ connected a.s.},
\qquad
\mathbb E\deg_R(o)\le2\varepsilon.
\tag{8}
\]

Then Bernoulli cost is \(1+\beta _1^{(2)}(\Gamma)\), every free pmp action has that cost, and the target group-cost equality follows.

**Proof.** Orient \(R\) using fresh continuous labels and partition oriented edges by group displacement. This is a graphing \(\Phi_R\) with

\[
C(\Phi_R)
 \le \frac12\mathbb E\deg_R(o)
 \le\varepsilon.
\]

Connectivity gives
\(\mathcal S_F\vee\Phi_R=\mathcal R\); hence

\[
C(\mathcal R)
 \le 1+\beta _1^{(2)}(\Gamma)+\varepsilon.
\]

Use (1), let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), then apply Abért–Weiss maximality. ∎

A long edge \(\{x,gx\}\) counts as one graphing edge because it is one partial orbit isomorphism. Expanding it into a word path in the original Cayley graph would require counting every path edge; no such uncharged expansion is used.

## 4. The exact blocker

**[PROVED_HERE, CONDITIONAL ON H77] Theorem 4.1.**

\[
\operatorname{relCost}(\mathcal R;\mathcal S_F)
 =\inf_R\frac12\mathbb E\deg_R(o),
\tag{9}
\]

where the infimum is over equivariant measurable orbit-edge processes for which \(F\cup R\) is connected almost surely.

**Proof.** A repair, oriented and partitioned by displacement, gives a relative graphing of cost at most half its expected degree.

Conversely, let
\(\Phi=(\phi_i:A_i\to B_i)\) satisfy
\(\mathcal S_F\vee\Phi=\mathcal R\). On the conull free part of the action, for every \(x\in A_i\) there is a unique displacement \(g_i(x)\in\Gamma\) with

\[
\phi_i(x)=g_i(x)x.
\]

Partitioning each domain by its countably many displacement values is Borel. Put the corresponding undirected edges on each orbit. They connect the FUSF components, and

\[
\mathbb E\deg_R(o)
 \le2\sum_i\mu(A_i)
 =2C(\Phi).
\]

Taking infima proves (9). ∎

**[STRONGER_BLOCKER]** For general \(\beta _1^{(2)}\), equation (B) implies fixed price at the lower bound. The converse does not follow from (3), because \(C(\mathcal S_F)>1\). Thus this repair demand is stronger than the stated infimum-cost target unless an additional relative-cost theorem is proved.

**[EQUIVALENT_BLOCKER] Theorem 4.2.** If
\(\beta _1^{(2)}(\Gamma)=0\), then, conditional on H77,

\[
(B)
\iff C(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{Ber}})=1
\iff \Gamma\text{ has fixed price }1.
\tag{10}
\]

**Proof.** Here \(C(\mathcal S_F)=1\) and its classes are infinite, so (4) gives

\[
\operatorname{relCost}(\mathcal R;\mathcal S_F)
 =C(\mathcal R)-1.
\]

Abért–Weiss gives the final equivalence.

There is also a direct construction. Give vertices fresh Bernoulli-\(\delta\) marks and let \(A\) be the event that the root is marked. Every infinite FUSF component contains a mark almost surely, so \(A\) is a complete section for \(\mathcal S_F\).

If Bernoulli cost is one, (5) gives

\[
C(\mathcal R|A,\mu_A)=1.
\]

Choose a graphing of \(\mathcal R|A\) of normalized cost below \(1+\eta\); in ambient measure it costs below
\(\delta(1+\eta)\). Together with \(\mathcal S_F\), it generates \(\mathcal R\), because each FUSF component can first move to a marked point. Convert it to orbit edges using Theorem 4.1 and let \(\delta\downarrow0\). ∎

## 5. Required stress tests

**[KNOWN/PROVED_HERE: free groups]** In the standard free-basis Cayley graph of \(F_r\), the graph is a tree, so FUSF is the whole graph and \(R=\varnothing\). Its cost is

\[
r=1+\beta _1^{(2)}(F_r).
\]

By contrast, on \(F_2=\langle a,b\rangle\), the deterministic forest containing only \(a\)-edges is a \(0\)-dependent forest of bi-infinite lines and has treeing cost one. Any repair of expected degree below two would give a graphing of the full \(F_2\)-relation of cost below two, contradicting fixed price two. Therefore “FIID/finite dependence plus infinite components” does not imply cheap repair. This counterexample is not FUSF. 

**[KNOWN plus Theorem 4.2: \(F_2\times F_2\)]** The Künneth formula gives
\(\beta _1^{(2)}=0\), and Khezeli proves fixed price one for every product of two infinite countable groups. Thus the conditional-H77 arbitrary-orbit-edge repair exists.

Yet FUSF=WUSF when \(\beta _1^{(2)}=0\), and the same BLPS implication applies here: on a nonamenable Cayley graph sufficiently sparse independent Bernoulli bond sprinkling does not provide the repair. Hence the abstract repair is not sparse independent Cayley-edge Bernoulli sprinkling; it may require dependence or adaptivity, long edges, or both. 

**[KNOWN plus Theorem 4.2: property \((T)\)]** Infinite property-\((T)\) groups have
\(\beta _1^{(2)}=0\). Hutchcroft–Pete prove group cost one, but fixed price one remains open. Thus the primary target is already known for this class, while the regular-iid FUSF repair is exactly its open fixed-price-one problem.

To distinguish the statements, choose free actions with costs tending to one and take their countable product. Lifting coordinate graphings makes that product action cost one. After adjoining the iid FUSF, the complete-section proof gives a cheap repair there, but this generally is not a regular-Bernoulli factor. 

**[KNOWN/OPEN: disconnected FUSF]** Pete–Timár exhibit different Cayley graphs of the same virtually free groups with connected and disconnected FUSF. They explicitly leave open finite versus infinite component touching and arbitrarily small invariant repair. 

## 6. A valid multiscale repair mechanism

**[PROVED_HERE] Theorem 6.1 (infinite-contact exhaustion).** Let \(P_0\) be an equivariant regular-iid random partition of \(\Gamma\). For each \(n\), let
\(K_n\subset\Gamma\setminus\{e\}\) be finite symmetric and let \(G_n\) have edges

\[
\{x,xk\},\qquad k\in K_n.
\]

Define \(P_{n+1}\) as the equivalence closure of pairs of \(P_n\)-blocks joined by infinitely many \(G_n\)-edges. Assume every pair of vertices lies in one \(P_n\)-block for some finite \(n\). Then, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a regular-iid equivariant edge process \(R\) such that, after regarding each \(P_0\)-block as internally connected, adding \(R\) makes the graph connected and

\[
\mathbb E\deg_R(o)<2\varepsilon.
\tag{11}
\]

**Proof.** Let \(d_n=|K_n|\) and choose \(q_n\in(0,1)\) with

\[
\sum_n q_n^2d_n<2\varepsilon.
\]

At stage \(n\), independently mark vertices with probability \(q_n\), using a fresh iid coordinate, and retain every \(G_n\)-edge whose two endpoints are marked.

Between two \(P_n\)-blocks joined by infinitely many \(G_n\)-edges, bounded degree yields an infinite matching. Along that matching, the two-endpoint marking events are independent and have probability \(q_n^2>0\), so at least one retained edge occurs almost surely. There are countably many stages and block pairs. Inductively, all \(P_{n+1}\)-blocks are connected; the exhaustion assumption gives global connectivity. Finally,

\[
\mathbb E\deg_R(o)
 \le\sum_nq_n^2d_n
 <2\varepsilon.
\]

Countably many fresh coordinates per vertex are encoded in one uniform label. ∎

A one-stage corollary says that a connected component quotient with infinitely many contacts across every adjacent pair is repairable at arbitrarily small density. This is a genuine sufficient condition, not “closeness implies connectedness.”

**[OPEN]** No such infinite-contact exhaustion is known for every FUSF. Pete–Timár’s examples leave open even the first finite-versus-infinite touching interface. The \(a\)-line forest on \(F_2\) fails the condition, consistently with its positive cost obstruction.

## Conclusion

The target is not proved or disproved. The smallest unclosed statement inside the assigned regular-iid FUSF route is (B). At
\(\beta _1^{(2)}=0\), it is exactly fixed price one; in general it is a stronger relative-cost assertion. The multiscale theorem proves that infinite-contact geometry would close the route, but that geometry remains unproved.
