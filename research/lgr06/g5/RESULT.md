# INCOMPLETE

The universal statement

\[
\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)=1+\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma)
\tag{1}
\]

is neither proved nor disproved here. Here \(\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)\) is the infimum of the costs of orbit relations over **all essentially free p.m.p. actions** of \(\Gamma\), not the cost of the Bernoulli action and not fixed price. This remains a general open problem; recent work in June 2026 proves it for one-relator groups, not universally. 

What follows is a complete derivation of:

1. an exact invariant-connected-graph variational formula for group cost;
2. an exact \(L^2\)-cycle defect;
3. a sharp obstruction to exact and weak-limit connectivity constructions;
4. a FUSF exchange formulation whose remaining step is precisely identified.

## 1. Connected invariant graph laws compute group cost

Let \(\mathscr C_\Gamma\) be the set of \(\Gamma\)-invariant laws on simple connected spanning graphs on vertex set \(\Gamma\), with finite expected degree at \(e\).

### PROVED_HERE

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)
=
\frac12\inf_{\nu\in\mathscr C_\Gamma}
\mathbb E_\nu \deg_G(e).
}
\tag{2}
\]

### Proof

Let \(\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)\) be free and let \(\Phi\) be a generating graphing. For \(x\in X\), identify its orbit with \(\Gamma\) by

\[
g\longleftrightarrow g^{-1}x.
\]

Join \(g,h\in\Gamma\) when a graphing edge of \(\Phi\) joins \(g^{-1}x\) and \(h^{-1}x\). Collapse loops and repeated edges. The resulting graph \(G_x\) is connected, and

\[
G_{\gamma x}=\gamma G_x,
\]

so its distribution is left-invariant. Every occurrence of the root in the domain or range of a graphing generator contributes at most one root incidence. Hence

\[
\mathbb E\deg_{G_x}(e)
\le
\sum_{\phi\in\Phi}
\bigl(\mu(\operatorname{dom}\phi)+\mu(\operatorname{ran}\phi)\bigr)
=
2C(\Phi).
\]

Taking the infimum over graphings and then over free actions gives

\[
\frac12\inf_{\nu\in\mathscr C_\Gamma}\mathbb E_\nu\deg(e)
\le \operatorname{cost}(\Gamma).
\]

Conversely, take \(G\sim\nu\) and, independently, iid atomless labels
\(u=(u_g)_{g\in\Gamma}\). The diagonal action on

\[
(\mathcal G_\Gamma\times[0,1]^\Gamma,\nu\otimes\lambda^\Gamma)
\]

is essentially free. For each \(\gamma\ne e\), define

\[
A_\gamma
=
\{(G,u):\{e,\gamma\}\in G,\ u_e<u_\gamma\}
\]

and

\[
\phi_\gamma(G,u)=\gamma^{-1}(G,u).
\]

These partial transformations and their inverses generate the full orbit relation because \(G\) is connected. Conditional on \(G\), each edge incident to \(e\) is oriented outward with probability \(1/2\). Thus

\[
C(\{\phi_\gamma\})
=
\sum_{\gamma\ne e}\mu(A_\gamma)
=
\frac12\mathbb E_\nu\deg_G(e).
\]

This proves the reverse inequality and hence (2). Involutions cause no exception: the partial transformation sends the chosen orientation domain to its opposite-oriented range. ∎

Equation (2) concerns **group cost**, since the diagonal action is merely some free action. A connected graph constructed as a factor of the Bernoulli action would give the stronger Bernoulli-cost bound. For finitely generated groups, Abért–Weiss show that Bernoulli and free FIID actions have maximal cost among free actions; equality with the Betti lower bound there would therefore prove fixed price. 

## 2. The exact cycle-space defect

Let \(\mathcal R\) be an aperiodic p.m.p. equivalence relation, and let \(H\) be a simple connected finite-cost graphing of \(\mathcal R\). Let

\[
C^{(2)}_1(H)
\]

be its oriented-edge Hilbert \(\mathcal R\)-module, with boundary operator \(\partial_1\). Define \(\mathcal B_H\) to be the closed \(\mathcal R\)-submodule generated by the signed chains of all finite leafwise cycles.

### PROVED_HERE

\[
\boxed{
C(H)
=
1+\beta^{(2)}_1(\mathcal R)
+\dim_{\mathcal R}\mathcal B_H.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\mathcal B_H=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H\text{ is a treeing a.e.}
\tag{4}
\]

### Proof

The edge module has von Neumann dimension

\[
\dim_{\mathcal R}C^{(2)}_1(H)=C(H),
\]

while the vertex module has dimension \(1\). Because the classes are infinite and the leafwise graphs are connected, reduced \(H^{(2)}_0\) vanishes. Therefore

\[
\dim_{\mathcal R}\overline{\operatorname{im}\partial_1}=1
\]

and dimension additivity gives

\[
\dim_{\mathcal R}\ker\partial_1=C(H)-1.
\tag{5}
\]

Attach a 2-cell along every finite leafwise combinatorial loop. The finite graphing words form a countable Borel family; after subdivision this is a measured simplicial \(\mathcal R\)-complex. Every leaf becomes simply connected, because every graph loop is a finite combinatorial loop and has received a filling. Its closed \(\partial_2\)-image is exactly \(\mathcal B_H\).

Gaboriau’s invariance theorem for sufficiently connected measured complexes therefore identifies the reduced first homology of this complex with the first \(L^2\)-homology of \(\mathcal R\):

\[
\beta^{(2)}_1(\mathcal R)
=
\dim_{\mathcal R}
\left(
\ker\partial_1/\mathcal B_H
\right).
\tag{6}
\]

Combining (5), (6), and dimension additivity proves (3). For non-uniformly bounded graphings, take a bounded-degree Borel exhaustion and use continuity of von Neumann dimension from below, as in the standard extended measured-\(L^2\) construction. Gaboriau’s theorem also identifies relation and group \(L^2\)-Betti numbers for free actions. 

If \(H\) has cycles on a non-null family of classes, then the set of vertices lying on finite cycles has positive measure: the saturation of a null set under a countable p.m.p. relation remains null. Choosing measurably a shortest cycle through such a vertex produces a nonzero element of \(\mathcal B_H\). A nonzero closed Hilbert submodule has positive dimension because the von Neumann trace is faithful. Conversely, a treeing has no finite cycle chains. This proves (4). ∎

This refines the known inequality

\[
C(\mathcal R)-1\ge\beta^{(2)}_1(\mathcal R),
\]

and is consistent with Gaboriau’s results that treeings attain cost and a finite-cost graphing attaining the relation cost must be a treeing. 

## 3. Exact Betti-degree attainment is equivalent to treeability

### STRONGER_BLOCKER — PROVED_HERE

For an infinite finitely generated group \(\Gamma\), the following are equivalent:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{there exists }\nu\in\mathscr C_\Gamma
\text{ with }
\mathbb E_\nu\deg(e)
=
2+2\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma);
\\
&\Gamma\text{ is treeable.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

### Proof

Suppose the invariant connected graph law exists. The diagonal free action constructed in §1 has a generating graphing \(H\) with

\[
C(H)=1+\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma).
\]

For a free action,

\[
\beta^{(2)}_1(\mathcal R_H)=\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma).
\]

Equation (3) therefore forces

\[
\dim_{\mathcal R_H}\mathcal B_H=0.
\]

Thus \(H\) is a treeing and \(\Gamma\) is treeable.

Conversely, suppose \(\Gamma\) has a free action with treeing \(T\). Gaboriau’s treeing equality gives

\[
C(T)=1+\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma).
\]

Applying the orbit-coordinate construction from §1 produces an invariant spanning-tree law on \(\Gamma\) of mean degree

\[
2C(T)=2+2\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma).
\]

This proves (7). ∎

Thus a non-treeable group admits no exact connected minimizer at the Betti degree budget, even with arbitrary invariant randomness, arbitrary long edges, and output not constrained to be determinantal.

### STRONGER_BLOCKER — minimizing weak limits

Let \(\nu_n\in\mathscr C_\Gamma\) satisfy

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_n}\deg(e)
\longrightarrow
2+2\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma)
\]

and suppose \(\nu_n\Rightarrow\nu\). An appropriate hypothesis for passing the mean degree to the limit is not ordinary uniform integrability of the numerical degrees, but **incident-edge tightness**:

\[
\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_n
\sum_{\gamma\notin K_j}
\nu_n\bigl(\{e,\gamma\}\in G\bigr)=0
\tag{8}
\]

for a finite exhaustion \(K_j\uparrow\Gamma\).

Under (8), coordinatewise weak convergence and truncation of the countable sum give

\[
\mathbb E_\nu\deg(e)
=
2+2\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma).
\]

If \(\Gamma\) is non-treeable, \(\nu\) therefore cannot be almost surely connected by (7).

A common finite edge range makes (8) automatic. A mere uniform degree bound does not: a single incident edge may move to group elements tending to infinity and disappear from the product-topology limit. This is the precise obstruction to an unsupported compactness argument.

### Calibration

**KNOWN — free groups.** For \(F_r\), the standard Cayley tree has degree

\[
2r=2+2(r-1),
\]

so the exact minimizer exists.

**KNOWN — \(F_2\times F_2\).** It has \(\beta^{(2)}_1=0\), group cost \(1\), indeed fixed price \(1\), but is non-treeable. Consequently the infimum of connected invariant mean degrees is \(2\), yet no connected invariant law attains degree \(2\). 

**KNOWN — property (T).** Every infinite property-(T) group has \(\beta^{(2)}_1=0\), group cost \(1\), and is non-treeable. Hutchcroft–Pete’s theorem proves group cost \(1\), not fixed price \(1\), which remains a separate issue. Again the degree infimum is \(2\) and is not attained. 

For the latter two classes, FUSF cannot be connected: a connected invariant forest would itself be an invariant spanning-tree law and would make the group treeable.

These examples rule out exact minimizers and naive connectivity-preserving weak limits. They do not disprove (1).

## 4. FUSF exchange normal form

Fix a finite Cayley graph of \(\Gamma\), and let \(F\) denote its free uniform spanning forest. The known degree identity is

\[
C(F)
=
\frac12\mathbb E\deg_F(e)
=
1+\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma).
\tag{9}
\]

The original Lyons–Gaboriau route asks for a connected invariant augmentation with arbitrarily small added mean degree. 

The infimum defining group cost is attained by a free action: take a diagonal product of actions whose costs converge to the infimum.  Let

\[
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X_0,\mu_0)
\]

be such an action. Form its product with the FUSF \(\Gamma\)-space. The product remains free and factors onto \(X_0\). Hence

\[
C(\mathcal R_*)\le C(\mathcal R_{X_0})
=\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma).
\]

The reverse inequality follows from the definition of group cost, so

\[
C(\mathcal R_*)=\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma).
\tag{10}
\]

Inside \(\mathcal R_*\), \(F\) is now a measurable forest subgraphing, generally not a generating graphing.

Let \(H\) be any simple finite-cost generating graphing of \(\mathcal R_*\), and define

\[
D_H:=F\setminus H,
\qquad
A_H:=H\setminus F.
\]

Then

\[
H=(F\setminus D_H)\cup A_H,
\]

and additivity of edge measure gives

\[
C(H)-C(F)=C(A_H)-C(D_H).
\tag{11}
\]

Combining (3) and (9) yields:

### PROVED_HERE — exact repair defect

\[
\boxed{
C(A_H)-C(D_H)
=
\dim_{\mathcal R_*}\mathcal B_H
\ge 0.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus the amount by which additions fail to be paid for by deleted forest edges is exactly the von Neumann dimension of the repaired graphing’s finite-cycle module.

Taking the infimum over all generating \(H\) gives:

### EQUIVALENT_BLOCKER

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)-1-\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma)
=
\inf_{H\text{ generating}}
\bigl(C(A_H)-C(D_H)\bigr).
}
\tag{13}
\]

Consequently, the primary target is equivalent to the following concrete surgery statement:

> For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exist measurable edge sets
> \[
> D\subseteq F,\qquad A\cap F=\varnothing
> \]
> such that
> \[
> H=(F\setminus D)\cup A
> \]
> generates \(\mathcal R_*\) and
> \[
> C(A)\le C(D)+\varepsilon.
> \tag{14}
> \]

This is strictly less restrictive than adding an \(\varepsilon\)-cost connector, which is the special case \(D=\varnothing\). It allows forest edges to be deleted in exchange for connecting edges.

An exact balanced repair,

\[
C(A)=C(D),
\]

would force \(\mathcal B_H=0\), hence would produce a treeing. It is therefore impossible for \(F_2\times F_2\) and for infinite property-(T) groups. In those known-cost-one classes there are nevertheless abstract sequences of graphings with positive defects tending to zero. There is no uniform positive defect; the phenomenon is nonattainment.

A one-ended spanning-subforest theorem by itself does not close (14): one-endedness controls component geometry but gives no estimate on \(C(A)-C(D)\) or on \(\dim\mathcal B_H\). 

## 5. Exact orientation interface for H77

### PROVED_HERE — conditional on H77

H77 does imply that FUSF on a finite Cayley graph is a factor of regular \(\Gamma\)-iid labels despite orientation signs. It does not supply a connector or an exchange satisfying (14).

Let

\[
\vec E\cong\Gamma\times S
\]

be the directed Cayley arcs. This is a finite union of free regular \(\Gamma\)-orbits, including when some generator is an involution.

On \(\ell^2(\vec E)\), let

\[
\mathcal A=\{f:f(\bar a)=-f(a)\}
\]

be the antisymmetric subspace. Let \(P_F\) be the orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement of the closed finite-cycle space inside \(\mathcal A\), extended by zero on \(\mathcal A^\perp\). This is a positive contraction commuting with the pure arc-permutation action. The corresponding unoriented DPP is FUSF. 

Apply H77 to \(P_F\). The columns indexed by \(a\) and \(\bar a\) are negatives, so

\[
\det P_F[\{a,\bar a\}]=0.
\]

Thus almost surely at most one orientation of an undirected edge is selected.

For a finite unoriented edge set \(B\), choose one arc \(a_e\) for each \(e\in B\). For any orientation choices \(\epsilon_e\in\{\pm1\}\),

\[
\det P_F[(a_e^{\epsilon_e})_{e\in B}]
=
2^{-|B|}\det K_F[B].
\tag{15}
\]

Indeed, the antisymmetric component of an arc basis vector has norm \(2^{-1/2}\), while reversing an arc only conjugates the kernel matrix by diagonal signs. The \(2^{|B|}\) orientation events are disjoint, so summing (15) over them gives

\[
\mathbb P(B\subseteq\pi(Y))
=
\det K_F[B].
\]

Hence projection of the arc process onto unoriented edges is exactly FUSF.

Finally, one uniform label at \(g\in\Gamma\) Borel-decodes the finitely many independent labels indexed by \((g,s)\), yielding regular-\(\Gamma\) iid input. This proves the interface, conditional on H77. It gives no monotone coupling, finitary code, connected output, or bound toward (14).

## 6. Exact remaining step

### OPEN — smallest unclosed step

Prove (14) for every infinite finitely generated group, or exhibit a group for which the infimum in (13) is positive.

The former proves

\[
\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)=1+\beta^{(2)}_1(\Gamma).
\]

The latter gives a counterexample.

Requiring \(A,D\) and the resulting connected graphing to be produced inside the Bernoulli action would be stronger than the primary target: by Abért–Weiss maximality, it would force every free action to have the same cost.

The exact contribution here is therefore not a resolution of the conjecture, but:

\[
\text{repair excess}
=
\text{finite-cycle }L^2\text{-dimension},
\]

together with the proof that exact minimizers are possible precisely for treeable groups and the balanced-exchange formulation (14) for approximate minimizers.
