# Independent adversarial audit of the invariant-graph LG response

STATUS: **CORRECT**

## Scope and boundary

No critical mathematical gap was found in the response's stated results. The verdict covers the invariant connected-graph formula (2), the cycle-defect formula (3)--(4), exact attainment and weak-limit obstruction (7)--(8), the FUSF exchange identities (9)--(14), and the directed-arc realization conditional on H77.

The verdict does not change the response's status **INCOMPLETE**. In particular:

- the universal Lyons--Gaboriau equality is neither proved nor disproved;
- the directed-arc factor construction remains conditional on H77;
- the exchange condition (14) is an equivalent blocker, not a proved weaker theorem;
- no Bernoulli-cost or fixed-price conclusion follows from the group-cost infimum construction;
- no novelty claim is certified.

## 1. Invariant connected graphs and group cost

The two inequalities in (2) have the correct scope.

Starting with an arbitrary essentially free p.m.p. action and a generating graphing, orbit coordinates produce a left-invariant law on connected simple spanning graphs of \(\Gamma\). Collapsing loops and repeated edges cannot increase degree. Since every partial graphing generator contributes at most one root incidence from its domain and one from its range,
\[
\mathbb E\deg_G(e)\le 2C(\Phi).
\]
This gives the first inequality after taking both infima.

Conversely, for an arbitrary invariant connected law \(\nu\), adjoining independent atomless labels makes the diagonal action essentially free. Orient each incident edge by the label comparison and retain one partial transformation for the chosen orientation. Each undirected root edge contributes exactly one half to the expected graphing cost, while connectedness of the sampled graph implies generation of the full orbit relation. Thus the constructed free action has a generating graphing of cost
\[
\frac12\mathbb E_\nu\deg_G(e).
\]
The involution case is harmless: the selected domain is carried to the opposite orientation and the inverse partial map supplies the reverse step.

Therefore
\[
\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)
=\frac12\inf_{\nu\in\mathscr C_\Gamma}\mathbb E_\nu\deg_G(e)
\]
is indeed the infimal group cost. It is not the maximal Bernoulli cost. This characterization also appears explicitly as equation (1.1) in Hutchcroft--Pete.

## 2. Exact \(L^2\)-cycle defect

Formula (3) is correct under the standard measured-chain conventions. Here \(C_1^{(2)}(H)\) is the antisymmetric oriented-edge module, so each undirected graphing edge is counted once and
\[
\dim_{\mathcal R}C_1^{(2)}(H)=C(H).
\]

When the graphing has finite expected degree but no uniform degree bound, \(\partial_1\) must be read as the closed densely defined incidence operator obtained from the finite-chain core, equivalently through a standard uniformly locally bounded exhaustion. Its polar decomposition gives Murray--von Neumann rank-nullity with the closure of the range. Since \(H\) connects every aperiodic class, reduced \(H_0^{(2)}\) vanishes, and hence
\[
\dim_{\mathcal R}\overline{\operatorname{im}\partial_1}=1,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathcal R}\ker\partial_1=C(H)-1.
\]
Finite expected degree also implies finite leaf degree almost surely, although the degrees need not be uniformly bounded.

Attach 2-cells along the countable Borel family of finite leafwise loops. Each leaf becomes simply connected. The closed image of \(\partial_2\) is exactly the closed module \(\mathcal B_H\) generated by the signed finite-cycle chains. Gaboriau's invariance theorem therefore gives
\[
\beta_1^{(2)}(\mathcal R)
=\dim_{\mathcal R}(\ker\partial_1/\mathcal B_H).
\]
Dimension additivity proves
\[
C(H)=1+\beta_1^{(2)}(\mathcal R)+\dim_{\mathcal R}\mathcal B_H.
\]
The response's brief appeal to the extended measured-\(L^2\) construction is sufficient once the boundary operator and all images are interpreted with these closures; treating \(\partial_1\) as an everywhere-defined bounded operator would be incorrect, but the proof does not require that interpretation.

If finite cycles occur on a non-null family of classes, measurably choosing a shortest one gives a nonzero vector in \(\mathcal B_H\); faithfulness of the finite trace makes its closed generated submodule have positive dimension. Conversely, a treeing has no finite cycle chain. This proves (4).

## 3. Exact attainment and weak limits

If a connected invariant graph law has mean degree \(2+2\beta_1^{(2)}(\Gamma)\), the free diagonal action from Section 1 has a generating graphing of cost \(1+\beta_1^{(2)}(\Gamma)\). Formula (3) forces its finite-cycle module to vanish, so it is a treeing. Conversely, a free treeable action yields an invariant spanning-tree law of exactly that mean degree. Thus (7) has both directions and is equivalent to treeability.

The weak-limit statement uses the right tightness hypothesis. Product-topology convergence controls every fixed edge coordinate, while
\[
\sup_n\sum_{\gamma\notin K_j}
\nu_n(\{e,\gamma\}\in G)\longrightarrow0
\]
prevents degree mass from escaping to group elements tending to infinity. Truncation then passes the expected degree to the limit. Ordinary uniform integrability of the numerical random variables \(\deg(e)\) would not suffice: a bounded number of incident edges can escape through their labels while the numerical degree remains bounded.

For a non-treeable group, the limiting law at the exact Betti degree therefore cannot be almost surely connected. This statement neither asserts that the weak limit is connected nor uses closed \(L^2\)-span as a replacement for graph connectivity.

The calibrations are also correct:

- the standard Cayley tree realizes equality for free groups;
- \(F_2\times F_2\) has cost one and \(\beta_1^{(2)}=0\), but is non-treeable, so degree two is an unattained connected-law infimum;
- Hutchcroft--Pete prove group cost one for every countably infinite property-(T) group and explicitly leave fixed price one open;
- the June 2026 one-relator preprint proves \(\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)-1=\beta_1^{(2)}(\Gamma)\) for every infinite one-relator group in its stated normal form. It is a special-case result, not a universal resolution.

## 4. FUSF exchange normal form

The group-cost infimum is attained by a free action: take a diagonal product of free actions whose costs decrease to the infimum. Projection to each free factor is class-bijective on every orbit, so graphings lift without increasing cost. The product action therefore has cost exactly \(\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)\).

Taking its product with the FUSF space again gives a free extension and does not increase the cost. The reverse inequality is definitional, hence the resulting relation \(\mathcal R_*\) has cost exactly \(\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)\). This is an arbitrary cost-minimizing free action carrying a measurable FUSF subgraphing; it is not asserted to be Bernoulli.

For any simple finite-cost generating graphing \(H\) of \(\mathcal R_*\), set
\[
D_H=F\setminus H,\qquad A_H=H\setminus F.
\]
Edge-measure additivity gives
\[
C(H)-C(F)=C(A_H)-C(D_H).
\]
Applying the cycle-defect formula to \(H\), using
\(C(F)=1+\beta_1^{(2)}(\Gamma)\), gives exactly
\[
C(A_H)-C(D_H)=\dim_{\mathcal R_*}\mathcal B_H\ge0.
\]
Taking the infimum over generating \(H\) proves (13).

Consequently, condition (14) is equivalent to
\[
\operatorname{cost}(\Gamma)=1+\beta_1^{(2)}(\Gamma).
\]
It is genuinely less restrictive than demanding an added connector of vanishing cost because it permits deletion of FUSF edges, but it is not mathematically weaker than the original LG target. The response labels it `EQUIVALENT_BLOCKER`, which is the correct classification.

No step infers connectivity from equality of closed \(L^2\) spans. The exchange identity applies only after \(H\) is separately required to generate \(\mathcal R_*\). This is the essential logical guardrail.

An exactly balanced repair would make \(\mathcal B_H=0\) and hence produce a treeing. Its impossibility for the stated non-treeable cost-one groups follows. When group cost equals the Betti lower bound, approximate minimizing graphings give positive defects tending to zero, so nonattainment does not imply a uniform positive defect.

## 5. Directed-arc H77 interface

For a Cayley graph arising from a finite generating set, the directed arc set is a finite union of free regular \(\Gamma\)-orbits, including the involution case. The phrase “finite Cayley graph” in the response should be read as this locally finite Cayley graph; the vertex set is of course infinite.

Let \(U\) embed one arbitrarily oriented copy of the unoriented edge space into the antisymmetric arc space by
\[
U\delta_e=2^{-1/2}(\delta_a-\delta_{\bar a}).
\]
If \(K_F\) is the usual FUSF projection, then the arc operator is
\(P_F=UK_FU^*\), extended by zero on the symmetric arc space. It is a positive equivariant contraction. The two columns belonging to \(a,\bar a\) are negatives, so the arc DPP selects at most one orientation of each edge.

For \(k=|B|\), compression to any orientation choice has determinant
\[
2^{-k}\det K_F[B];
\]
reversing arcs only conjugates the matrix by diagonal signs. The \(2^k\) orientation events are disjoint, and their sum gives \(\det K_F[B]\). Thus forgetting orientations produces exactly FUSF. Applying H77 to this regular arc action and splitting one uniform into finitely many independent uniforms gives the claimed iid-factor interface.

This interface supplies no connector, no monotone coupling, and no proof of (14), exactly as the response states.
