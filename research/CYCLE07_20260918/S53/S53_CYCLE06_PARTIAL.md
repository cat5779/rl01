## 1. Exact second derivative of the moving projection error

Fix \(\alpha\), a finite fine state space \(\Omega_M\), and the coarse sigma-field generated by \(Y_{J_R}\). Write
\[
p_h=p_{h,\alpha}^M,\qquad d_h=d_h^M,
\]
and equip functions on \(\Omega_M\) with the moving inner product
\[
\langle u,v\rangle_h
=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_M}p_h(\omega)u(\omega)v(\omega).
\]

Let \(V_R\) be the fixed vector space of \(Y_{J_R}\)-measurable functions. Let \(P_h\) denote orthogonal projection onto \(V_R\) in the moving \(L^2(p_h)\) metric. Define
\[
f_h=\frac{d_h}{p_h},\qquad
g_h=P_hf_h,\qquad
e_h=f_h-g_h.
\]

Exact marginalization gives
\[
g_h=f_{*,h,\alpha}^{J_R}
\]
and
\[
I_h^M(\alpha)-I_h^{J_R}(\alpha)
=\|e_h\|_h^2.
\]

At a base point \(h_0\), eventually \(h_0=1/2\), put
\[
s=\frac{p'}p,\qquad
t=\frac{p''}p,\qquad
u=\frac{d'}p,\qquad
v=\frac{d''}p.
\]
All quantities without an \(h\)-subscript are now evaluated at \(h_0\). Set
\[
k=u-sg,\qquad
z=(I-P)k,\qquad P=P_{h_0}.
\]

### Covariant Galerkin curvature identity

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^2}{dh^2}
\left(I_h^M(\alpha)-I_h^{J_R}(\alpha)\right)\Big|_{h_0}
={}&2\|z\|_p^2\\
&+2\left\langle e,\,v-tg-2sk\right\rangle_p\\
&+\left\langle e,(2s^2-t)e\right\rangle_p .
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1}
\]

Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
z=e'+se,\qquad z\perp_pV_R.
}
\tag{2}
\]

An equivalent form is
\[
\boxed{
D''=2\|e'+se\|_p^2
+2\langle e,f''\rangle_p
+\langle e,(t-2s^2)e\rangle_p,
}
\tag{3}
\]
where \(D=I^M-I^{J_R}\).

### Proof

The fine and coarse normal equations are
\[
\langle f,\varphi\rangle_p=\sum_\omega d(\omega)\varphi(\omega)
\quad\text{for arbitrary }\varphi,
\]
and
\[
\langle g,\varphi\rangle_p=\sum_\omega d(\omega)\varphi(\omega)
\quad\text{for }\varphi\in V_R.
\]
Thus
\[
e\perp_pV_R.
\]

Differentiating the coarse normal equation yields
\[
\langle g'+sg,\varphi\rangle_p=\langle u,\varphi\rangle_p,
\qquad \varphi\in V_R,
\]
hence
\[
g'=P(u-sg)=Pk.
\tag{4}
\]

Since
\[
f'=u-sf,
\]
we obtain
\[
e'+se=f'-g'+se=k-Pk=(I-P)k=z.
\]
This proves (2). In particular, the derivative of the moving conditional-expectation operator has not been suppressed: it is exactly contained in \(g'=Pk\).

For an optimized quadratic functional
\[
I_h=\sup_a\sum_\omega\{2d_h(\omega)a(\omega)-p_h(\omega)a(\omega)^2\},
\]
the finite-dimensional envelope identity is
\[
I_h''
=2\sum d_h''f_h-\sum p_h''f_h^2
+2\sum p_h(f_h')^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Applying the same identity to the coarse optimizer and lifting coarse functions to \(\Omega_M\) gives
\[
D''
=2\langle v,e\rangle_p
-\langle t(f+g),e\rangle_p
+2\bigl(\|f'\|_p^2-\|g'\|_p^2\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]

Now
\[
f'=g'+z-se.
\]
Because \(z\perp_pg'\), and because \(k=g'+z\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|f'\|_p^2-\|g'\|_p^2
&=\|z-se\|_p^2-2\langle g',se\rangle_p\\
&=\|z\|_p^2-2\langle sk,e\rangle_p+\|se\|_p^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Substitution into (6), followed by \(f=g+e\), proves (1).

Finally, differentiating \(d=pf\) twice gives
\[
v=tf+2sf'+f'',
\]
which reduces (1) to (3). ∎

After integrating over \(\alpha\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{x,E}''(1/2)-\mathcal F_{x,J_R}''(1/2)
=\frac14\int_0^1D_{E,R,\alpha}''(1/2)\,d\alpha.
}
\tag{8}
\]

This is the complete finite identity requested in the assignment. The law, the metric, the conditional expectation, both optimizers, and all normalization derivatives move.

## 2. Why the S44 first-order jet cannot by itself control this curvature

The term \(2\|z\|^2\) is the covariant first-jet residual. Equations (1) and (3), however, contain an additional signed forcing involving \(f''\), \(p''/p\), and the zeroth-order residual \(e\).

A two-atom example shows that this is a genuine obstruction to a general first-jet lemma.

Fix \(\alpha=1/2\), take
\[
p_h(+)=p_h(-)=\frac12,
\]
let the coarse sigma-field be trivial, and put
\[
d_h(\pm)=\pm\left(r+jh+\frac12ch^2\right).
\]
For \(|r|<1\) and sufficiently small \(h\),
\[
P_{1,h}=p_h+\frac12d_h,\qquad
P_{0,h}=p_h-\frac12d_h
\]
are valid probability laws.

The projection error is
\[
D(h)=4\left(r+jh+\frac12ch^2\right)^2,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
D''(0)=8(j^2+rc).
}
\]
The base residual and first jet depend only on \(r,j\), while the curvature can be changed arbitrarily through \(c\).

Thus:

- the ansatz “base \(L^2\) error plus first optimizer jet controls curvature” is false in general;
- this does not refute the sine-family claim;
- an actual second-order observable must be paid.

The rest of the proof constructs and pays that observable for the true sine law.

## 3. Exact normalization bridge

Let \(w_h^J\) be the actual law of \(Y_J\), and let
\[
q_h^J(z)=\mathbb P_h(Y_0=1\mid Y_J=z).
\]
Because the center marginal is exactly \(h\),
\[
P_{(h,1)}^J(z)
=w_h^J(z)\frac{q_h^J(z)}h,
\qquad
P_{(h,0)}^J(z)
=w_h^J(z)\frac{1-q_h^J(z)}{1-h}.
\tag{9}
\]

For \(a,b>0\),
\[
\int_0^1
\frac{(a-b)^2}{\alpha a+(1-\alpha)b}\,d\alpha
=(a-b)\log\frac ab.
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore \(4\mathcal F\) is exactly the Jeffreys divergence of the two conditional exterior laws. Substituting (9) gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{x,J}(h)
=\mathbb E_{w_h^J}\Phi(h,q_h^J),
}
\tag{11}
\]
where
\[
\Phi(h,q)
=\frac14\frac{q-h}{h(1-h)}
\left(
\log\frac q{1-q}-\log\frac h{1-h}
\right).
\tag{12}
\]

Define
\[
\ell(q)=\log\frac q{1-q},
\]
\[
\phi(q)=\left(q-\frac12\right)\ell(q),
\qquad
\chi(q)=2-4q-\ell(q).
\tag{13}
\]
At fixed \(q\),
\[
\Phi(1/2,q)=\phi(q),
\]
\[
\partial_h\Phi(1/2,q)=\chi(q),
\]
and
\[
\partial_h^2\Phi(1/2,q)=8+8\phi(q).
\tag{14}
\]

Put
\[
G_J(h)=\mathbb E_{w_h^J}\phi(q_h^J),
\qquad
H_J(h)=\mathbb E_{w_h^J}\chi(q_h^J).
\tag{15}
\]
Expanding the integrand first and only then differentiating the moving expectation yields
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{x,J}''(1/2)
=G_J''(1/2)+2H_J'(1/2)+8+8G_J(1/2).
}
\tag{16}
\]

This is the required correction to a frozen-\(G\) calculation. The identity
\[
\mathcal F_{x,J}(1/2)=G_J(1/2)
\]
does not permit differentiation unless the \(H'\) and \(8+8G\) terms are retained.

## 4. Exact finite-law interface with the complete S41 kernel

For a finite observation set \(E\), encode an exterior word by
\[
y_i\in\{+1,-1\},
\qquad
S_y=\operatorname{diag}(y_i).
\]
The exact atom law of the true principal marginal can be written
\[
w_h^E(y)=(-1)^{n_-(y)}\det B_h(y),
\]
where
\[
B_h(y)
=\left(h-\frac12\right)I
+\frac12S_y
+x\left(Q_E-\frac12I\right).
\tag{17}
\]
Thus
\[
B_h'=I.
\]

Let
\[
G_h=B_h^{-1},\qquad b=xQ_{E0},\qquad v=G_hb.
\]
The Schur posterior of the anchor is
\[
q_h=h-b^*G_hb.
\]
Define
\[
r_i=y_i-(G_h)_{ii},
\qquad
d_i=q_h(y^i)-q_h(y)
=-\frac{|v_i|^2}{r_i}.
\tag{18}
\]

At \(h=1/2\), the hidden-posterior representation turns the relevant kernel into an orthogonal projection \(P\), and
\[
q=\frac12+x\left(P_{00}-\frac12\right).
\tag{19}
\]
All one- and two-flip posteriors lie in
\[
\left[\frac{1-x}{2},\frac{1+x}{2}\right].
\]

For a smooth \(f\), define the Bregman remainder
\[
B_f(q,d)=f(q+d)-f(q)-f'(q)d
\]
and
\[
\mathcal B_{f,E}
=f'(q)+\sum_{i\in E}r_iB_f(q,d_i).
\tag{20}
\]

### Exact flip-generator derivative

For any differentiable word functional \(A_h(y)\), with a dot denoting the fixed-word derivative,
\[
\boxed{
\frac d{dh}\mathbb E_{w_h^E}A_h\Big|_{h=1/2}
=
\mathbb E\left[
\dot A+\sum_{i\in E}r_i\{A(y^i)-A(y)\}
\right].
}
\tag{21}
\]

Indeed,
\[
\frac{w_h'(y)}{w_h(y)}=\operatorname{Tr}G_h.
\]
The determinant lemma and Sherman–Morrison formula give
\[
\frac{w_h(y^i)}{w_h(y)}=-y_ir_i,
\qquad
r_i(y^i)=-\frac1{r_i(y)}.
\tag{22}
\]
Changing variables \(y\leftrightarrow y^i\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\,r_i\{A(y^i)-A(y)\}
&=\mathbb E\,(y_i-r_i)A\\
&=\mathbb E\,(G_h)_{ii}A.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing over \(i\) proves (21).

For \(A_h=f(q_h)\),
\[
\dot q=A_E:=1+\sum_{i\in E}|v_i|^2,
\qquad
r_id_i=-|v_i|^2.
\]
Hence
\[
A_E=1-\sum_i r_id_i,
\]
and (21) becomes
\[
\boxed{
\frac d{dh}\mathbb E_{w_h^E}f(q_h)\Big|_{1/2}
=\mathbb E\mathcal B_{f,E}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

This identity holds throughout a neighborhood with the corresponding \(h\)-dependent Schur variables, so it may legitimately be differentiated.

The fixed-word derivatives are
\[
\dot q=A_E,
\qquad
\dot r_i=R_{i,E}:=\sum_{k\in E}|G_{ki}|^2,
\qquad
\dot d_i=A_E^i-A_E,
\tag{24}
\]
where
\[
A_E^i=1+\sum_{k\in E}|v_k^i|^2.
\]

Writing
\[
\Delta_i=f'(q+d_i)-f'(q),
\]
application of (21) to \(\mathcal B_{f,E}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^2}{dh^2}\mathbb E f(q_h)\Big|_{1/2}
=\mathbb E\Big[{}&
f''(q)A_E
+\sum_iR_{i,E}B_f(q,d_i)
+A_E\sum_i r_iB_{f'}(q,d_i)\\
&+\sum_i r_i\Delta_i(A_E^i-A_E+1)\\
&+\sum_{i,j}r_j
 \{r_i^jB_f(q+d_j,d_i^j)-r_iB_f(q,d_i)\}
\Big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]

Now use
\[
B_{f'}(q,d_i)=\Delta_i-f''(q)d_i
\]
and
\[
\sum_i r_id_i=1-A_E.
\]
The first, third, and fourth groups in (25) reduce exactly to
\[
f''(q)
+\sum_i r_iB_{f'}(q,d_i)
+\sum_i r_i\{\Delta_iA_E^i-f''(q)d_iA_E\}.
\]

For \(f=\phi\), this gives precisely
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr K_{x,E}
={}&\phi''(q)
+\sum_{i\in E}r_iB_{\phi'}(q,d_i)
+\sum_{i\in E}R_{i,E}B_\phi(q,d_i)\\
&+\sum_{i\in E}r_i
\Big(
[\phi'(q+d_i)-\phi'(q)]A_E^i
-\phi''(q)d_iA_E
\Big)\\
&+\sum_{i,j\in E}r_j
\Big(
r_i^jB_\phi(q+d_j,d_i^j)
-r_iB_\phi(q,d_i)
\Big).
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Therefore the crucial source interface is established rather than assumed:
\[
\boxed{
G_E''(1/2)=\mathbb E\mathscr K_{x,E}.
}
\tag{27}
\]

No equality known only at \(h=1/2\) has been differentiated.

\[
T_0=\sum_{o\in O}|P_{o0}|^2,
\qquad
T_i=\sum_{o\in O}|P_{oi}|^2,
\]
\[
W_0=\sum_{i\in J}|P_{i0}|^2T_i,
\]
and
\[
S_i=\sum_{j\in J}|P_{ij}|^2|P_{j0}|^2,
\qquad
Z_0=\sum_{i\in J}T_iS_i.
\]
Put
\[
\widetilde U_0=T_0+W_0+Z_0.
\tag{28}
\]

Let
\[
\Lambda_x=\frac2{1-x},
\]
\[
C_{\rm tail}(x)=2^{12}\Lambda_x^{25},
\qquad
C_{\rm dir}(x)=2^{26}\Lambda_x^{36}.
\tag{29}
\]

\[
|\mathscr K_{x,E}-\mathscr K_{x,J}|
\le C_{\rm tail}(x)(T_0+W_0),
\tag{30}

and, for an outside diagonal-likelihood path \(P_t\) of speed

\left|\frac d{dt}\mathscr K_{x,J}(P_t)\right|
\le

\left(

\tag{31}

together with

\tag{32}
\]

Removing or adding the anchor observation changes \(\widetilde U_0\) by at most the factor

\tag{33}

## 6. New fixed-anchor mass-transport estimate

Let \(P^\star\) be the hidden posterior projection after all actual channel outputs, including the anchor, have been observed at \(h=1/2\). Its distribution is stationary.

Define
\[
T_a(L)=\sum_{|j-a|\ge L}|P^\star_{ja}|^2.
\]
The S44 number-variance estimate gives
\[
\mathbb ET_0(L)\le A_Q(L),
\qquad
A_Q(L):=\frac{V_Q(L)}L,
\tag{34}
\]
where
\[
V_Q(L)\le\frac{\log L+4}{\pi^2}.
\tag{35}
\]

Let
\[
I_R=\{-R,\ldots,R\},\qquad
O_R=\mathbb Z\setminus I_R
\]
and
\[
L_k(R)=\left\lfloor\frac Rk\right\rfloor+1.
\]
Set
\[
\upsilon(R)
=
A_Q(L_1(R))
+2A_Q(L_2(R))
+3A_Q(L_3(R)).
\tag{36}
\]

### Fixed-anchor repaired-energy lemma

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\widetilde U_0(P^\star;I_R)
\le\upsilon(R).
}
\tag{37}
\]

### Proof

The direct tail satisfies
\[
\mathbb ET_0\le A_Q(R+1).
\]

For \(W_0\), every path \(0\to i\to o\), with \(|o|>R\), has either
\[
|i|\ge L_2(R)
\]
or
\[
|o-i|\ge L_2(R).
\]
Hence
\[
W_0
\le T_0(L_2)
+\sum_i|P^\star_{i0}|^2T_i(L_2).
\tag{38}
\]
Stationarity and Tonelli give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\sum_i|P^\star_{i0}|^2T_i(L)
&=
\mathbb E\left[
T_0(L)\sum_i|P^\star_{0,-i}|^2
\right]\\
&=
\mathbb E[T_0(L)P^\star_{00}]\\
&\le\mathbb ET_0(L).
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
Thus
\[
\mathbb EW_0\le2A_Q(L_2).
\]

For \(Z_0\), expand paths \(0\to j\to i\to o\):
\[
Z_0
\le
\sum_{|o|>R}\sum_{i,j}
|P^\star_{oi}|^2|P^\star_{ij}|^2|P^\star_{j0}|^2.
\tag{40}
\]
At least one of
\[
|j|,\qquad |i-j|,\qquad |o-i|
\]
is at least \(L_3(R)\).

The long first edge contributes at most \(T_0(L_3)\).

The long middle edge contributes at most
\[
\sum_j|P_{j0}|^2T_j(L_3),
\]
whose expectation is at most \(A_Q(L_3)\) by (39).

For the long last edge,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E
\sum_{i,j}T_i(L)|P_{ij}|^2|P_{j0}|^2\\
&\quad=
\mathbb E\left[
T_0(L)
\sum_k|P_{0k}|^2
\sum_m|P_{km}|^2
\right]\\
&\quad=
\mathbb E\left[
T_0(L)\sum_k|P_{0k}|^2P_{kk}
\right]\\
&\quad\le\mathbb ET_0(L).
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb EZ_0\le3A_Q(L_3).
\]
Adding the three estimates proves (37). ∎

Since \(L_k(R)\ge(R+1)/k\),
\[
\boxed{
\upsilon(R)
\le
\frac{14\{\log(R+1)+4\}}{\pi^2(R+1)}.
}
\tag{42}
\]

Let \(P_R^-\) denote the hidden posterior after observing only \(Y_{J_R}\), with both the anchor and the outside unobserved. Starting from \(P^\star\), erase the outside observations and then erase the anchor. The outside Gronwall factor and anchor factor each cost at most \(s_x^4\), giving
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\widetilde U_0(P_R^-;I_R)
\le s_x^8\upsilon(R).
}
\tag{43}
\]

For the infinite outside erase, one first uses finite-support diagonal tilts. The likelihood operators remain uniformly bounded; strong convergence of the posterior projections, together with Tonelli and Fatou for the nonnegative energies, passes (43) to the full outside.

This estimate is uniform over every finite annulus \(E\setminus J_R\).

## 7. Bound for the complete \(G''\) term

Couple the exact finite laws by first observing \(J_R\), then revealing \(E\setminus J_R\) through a simultaneous diagonal-likelihood path. Its operator-norm length is at most
\[
\log s_x.
\]

At the endpoint, (30), (32), and (43) give
\[
C_{\rm tail}(x)s_x^{12}\upsilon(R)
\]
for the direct complete-kernel tail.

Along the path,
\[
\sqrt{T_0}+\sqrt{W_0}+\frac12\sqrt{Z_0}
\le\sqrt3\sqrt{\widetilde U_0}.
\]
Also,
\[
\int_0^1
(\log s_x)e^{2t\log s_x}\,dt
=\frac{s_x^2-1}{2}.
\]
Using Jensen,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
|G_E''(1/2)-G_{J_R}''(1/2)|
\le{}&
C_{\rm tail}(x)s_x^{12}\upsilon(R)\\
&+
\frac{\sqrt3}{2}
C_{\rm dir}(x)(s_x^2-1)s_x^4
\sqrt{\upsilon(R)}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{44}
\]

This is uniform in the finite superset \(E\).

## 8. Bound for the moving-reference correction \(H'\)

By (23),
\[
H_J'(1/2)=\mathbb E\mathcal B_{\chi,J}.
\tag{45}
\]

On the legal posterior interval, with \(\Lambda=\Lambda_x\),
\[
\|\chi''\|_\infty\le4\Lambda^2,
\qquad
\|\chi'''\|_\infty\le20\Lambda^3.
\tag{46}
\]
The posterior dictionary gives
\[
|d_i|\le\Lambda^3p_i^2,
\qquad
p_i=|P_{i0}|.
\tag{47}
\]

The omitted-coordinate Bregman tail therefore satisfies
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal B_{\chi,E}-\mathcal B_{\chi,J}|
\le2\Lambda^9T_0.
}
\tag{48}
\]

For a differentiable projection path, use
\[
|\dot q|\le\delta_0,
\qquad
|\dot r_i|\le2\Lambda e_i,
\]
and
\[
|\dot d_i|
\le2\Lambda^3p_ih_i
+2\Lambda^5p_i^2e_i,
\tag{49}
\]
where
\[

## 10. The explicit final bound

Combine the normalization identity (16), the \(G''\) estimate (44), twice the \(H'\) estimate (52), and the zeroth-order estimate (57).

Define
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
B_x(R)={}&
s_x^{12}
\left(C_{\rm tail}(x)+4\Lambda_x^9\right)
\upsilon(R)\\
&+
s_x^4(s_x^2-1)
\left(
\frac{\sqrt3}{2}C_{\rm dir}(x)
+5\sqrt2\,\Lambda_x^{11}
\right)
\sqrt{\upsilon(R)}\\
&+
\frac{32}{(1-x^2)^2}\eta_x(R).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{58}
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
\left|\mathcal F_{x,E}''(1/2)
-\mathcal F_{x,J_R}''(1/2)\right|
\le B_x(R)
}
\tag{59}
\]
for every finite \(E\supset J_R\).

Since
\[
\upsilon(R)
=O\!\left(\frac{\log R}{R}\right)
\]
and
\[
\eta_x(R)
=O_x\!\left(\frac{\log R}{R}\right),
\]
the dominant term in (58) is the directional square-root term:
\[
\boxed{
B_x(R)
=O_x\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log R}{R}}\right).
}
\tag{60}
\]

Because (59) is uniform over all finite supersets \(E\supset J_R\), the finite curvatures form a Cauchy directed net. This proves the claimed finite-curvature limit and the radius-\(R\) error bound.

The rate is weaker than the S44 completed-jet rate \(O_x(\log R/R)\). The loss comes from integrating a directional estimate linear in \(\sqrt{\widetilde U_0}\); no claim of optimality is made.
