## 1. Put the clock and entropy production in the same time normalization

Define the original-rate generator

\[
G_l=l(n-l)L_l
\]

and its time variable

\[
s_l(a)
=
\frac{\widehat\tau_l(a)}{l(n-l)}
=
-\frac{\log\theta_{n,l}(z(a,c))}{2(n-1)}.
\]

Let

\[
F_l(s)
=
D\!\left(
u_l e^{sG_l}r_l^{\max}\,\middle\|\,u_l
\right),
\qquad
\mathcal I_l^G(s)=-F_l'(s).
\]

Because

\[
F_l(s)=K_l(l(n-l)s),
\]

the two entropy productions satisfy

\[
\mathcal I_l^G(s)
=
l(n-l)\,
\mathcal I_l^L(l(n-l)s).
\]

At

\[
a_*=\frac{1-c}{2},
\]

direct differentiation gives

\[
z'(a_*)=0
\]

and

\[
z''(a_*)
=
\frac{32c}{(1-c)^4}.
\]

For \(c=19/20\),

\[
z_*=1521,
\qquad
z''(a_*)=4\,864\,000.
\]

Since \(z'(a_*)=0\),

\[
-\widehat\tau_l''(a_*)
=
\frac{z''(a_*)}{\gamma_{2,l}}
\partial_z\log\theta_{n,l}(z_*),
\]

whereas

\[
-s_l''(a_*)
=
\frac{z''(a_*)}{2(n-1)}
\partial_z\log\theta_{n,l}(z_*).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
[-\widehat\tau_l'']\mathcal I_l^L
&=
[-l(n-l)s_l'']
\frac{\mathcal I_l^G}{l(n-l)}\\
&=
[-s_l'']\mathcal I_l^G\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{z''}{2(n-1)}
\partial_z\log\theta_{n,l}\,
\mathcal I_l^G(s_l).
}
\end{aligned}
\]

This exact cancellation is the reusable mechanism. Separately bounding \(-\widehat\tau_l''\) and \(\mathcal I_l^L\) would retain artificial factors \(l(n-l)\); combining them first removes those factors completely.

## 2. Entropy production decreases in the correct, original-rate time

On the \(l\)-slice,

\[
G_l
=
\sum_{1\le i<j\le n}(T_{ij}-I),
\]

where \(T_{ij}\) transposes coordinates \(i,j\). When both sites are occupied or both are empty, the summand vanishes, so this is exactly \(l(n-l)L_l\).

For a positive density \(f\),

\[
\mathcal I_l^G(f)
=
\frac12
\sum_{i<j}
\mathbb E_{u_l}
\Psi(f,T_{ij}f),
\]

where

\[
\Psi(x,y)
=
(x-y)(\log x-\log y).
\]

The Hessian is

\[
\nabla^2\Psi(x,y)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
(x+y)/x^2&-(1/x+1/y)\\[2mm]
-(1/x+1/y)&(x+y)/y^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its diagonal entries are positive and its determinant is zero, so \(\Psi\) is jointly convex.

The generator \(G_l\) is invariant under coordinate permutations. Hence \(e^{tG_l}\) commutes with every \(T_{ij}\). Jensen’s inequality therefore gives

\[
\Psi(e^{tG_l}f,e^{tG_l}T_{ij}f)
\le
e^{tG_l}\Psi(f,T_{ij}f).
\]

Integration against the invariant measure \(u_l\) yields

\[
\mathcal I_l^G(e^{tG_l}f)
\le
\mathcal I_l^G(f).
\]

Thus \(s\mapsto\mathcal I_l^G(s)\) is nonincreasing.

If \(r_l^{\max}\) has zeros, apply this argument after a positive time and let that time decrease to zero. All state spaces are finite, so entropy and the semigroup are continuous at zero.

It follows that

\[
s\mathcal I_l^G(s)
\le
\int_0^s\mathcal I_l^G(t)\,dt
=
F_l(0)-F_l(s)
\le
F_l(0).
\]

Therefore, at the actual corrected clock,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_l^G(s_l)
\le
\frac{F_l(0)}{s_l}
=
\frac{2(n-1)F_l(0)}
{-\log\theta_{n,l}}.
}
\]

This verifies both the required monotonicity and the correct time normalization before using an \(F/t\) estimate.

## 3. Uniform initial entropy budget on every layer

Let

\[
m=\min(l,n-l).
\]

First take \(l=m\le k\). The projection-DPP inclusion identity gives

\[
q_m^{\max}(S)
=
\frac{\det P_S}{\binom{k}{m}}.
\]

A direct verification is available from Cauchy–Binet. If \(P=UU^*\) and \(U^*U=I_k\), then

\[
\det(U^*XU)
=
\sum_{|A|=k}
\det(P_A)\prod_{i\in A}x_i.
\]

Putting \(x_i=1+t_i\), the coefficient of \(\prod_{i\in S}t_i\) is both

\[
\sum_{A\supset S}\det P_A
\]

and, by

\[
\det(I_k+U^*TU)=\det(I_n+TP),
\]

the principal minor \(\det P_S\).

For the half-density cyclic projection,

\[
P_{ii}=\frac12.
\]

Hadamard’s inequality consequently gives

\[
\det P_S\le 2^{-m}.
\]

The density relative to the uniform \(m\)-slice law satisfies

\[
r_m^{\max}(S)
\le
\frac{\binom{2k}{m}}
{2^m\binom{k}{m}}.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\frac{\binom{2k}{m}}{\binom{k}{m}}
=
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{2k-j}{k-j}
\le4^m.
\]

Indeed, the displayed factors increase with \(j\), so the average of their first \(m\) logarithms is at most the average of all first \(k\); the product of all \(k\) factors is

\[
\binom{2k}{k}\le4^k.
\]

Thus

\[
\|r_m^{\max}\|_\infty\le2^m
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
F_m(0)\le m\log2.
}
\]

For \(l>k\), complementation reduces the layer to minority size \(m=n-l\) for the complementary projection \(I-P\), which also has diagonal \(1/2\). The same bound follows.

For \(l=1\),

\[
q_1^{\max}(\{i\})
=
\frac{P_{ii}}k
=
\frac1n,
\]

so this law is uniform. The same holds at \(l=n-1\), and \(l=0,n\) are singleton slices. Therefore the four endpoint contributions vanish exactly.

## 4. Uniform control of the clock’s logarithmic sensitivity

For \(2\le m\le k\), write

\[
Z_m(z)
=
\sum_j
\binom{k}{j}\binom{k}{m-j}z^j
\]

and

\[
A_{m-2}(z)
=
\sum_j
\binom{k-2}{j}
\binom{k-2}{m-2-j}z^j.
\]

Then

\[
\theta_m(z)
=
\frac{(z-1)^2A_{m-2}(z)}
{\alpha_mZ_m(z)},
\qquad
\alpha_m=\frac{m(m-1)}{k(k-1)}.
\]

### Upper bound on \(\partial_z\log\theta_m\)

Let \(J_Z\) and \(J_A\) denote indices under the normalized summands of \(Z_m\) and \(A_{m-2}\). Then

\[
\partial_z\log\theta_m
=
\frac2{z-1}
+
\frac{\mathbb EJ_A-\mathbb EJ_Z}{z}.
\]

The ratio between corresponding coefficients is

\[
w_j
=
\frac{
(k-j)(k-j-1)(m-j)(m-j-1)
}{
k^2(k-1)^2
}.
\]

This is nonincreasing in \(j\). The \(A\)-distribution is therefore the \(Z\)-distribution tilted by a decreasing weight. Hence

\[
\mathbb EJ_A-\mathbb EJ_Z
=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_Z(J,w_J)}
{\mathbb E_Z w_J}
\le0.
\]

It follows that

\[
\boxed{
\partial_z\log\theta_m(z)
\le
\frac2{z-1}.
}
\]

Positivity can also be proved coefficientwise. Put \(y=z-1\). Then

\[
Z_m(1+y)=\sum_{r=0}^m c_ry^r,
\qquad
c_r=\binom{k}{r}\binom{2k-r}{m-r},
\]

while

\[
y^2A_{m-2}(1+y)
=
\sum_{r=0}^m c_rq_ry^r
\]

with

\[
q_r
=
\frac{
r(r-1)(2k-m)(2k-m-1)
}{
k(k-1)(2k-r)(2k-r-1)
}.
\]

The sequence \(q_r\) increases with \(r\). If \(R\) has weights proportional to \(c_ry^r\), then

\[
\theta_m=\frac{\mathbb E q_R}{\alpha_m},
\qquad
y\partial_y\theta_m
=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(R,q_R)}
{\alpha_m}
\ge0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
0\le
\partial_z\log\theta_m(z)
\le
\frac2{z-1}.
}
\]

### Uniform positive lower bound on the clock

Let

\[
f(t)=(1+t)(1+zt)
\]

and

\[
a_j=[t^j]f(t)^{k-2},
\]

with negative-index coefficients set to zero. Differentiating \(f(t)^k\) twice and extracting the coefficient of \(t^{m-2}\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(z-1)^2}{\theta_m}
={}&
(z+1)^2+\frac{2z}{k-1}\\
&+
z(z+1)\left(4+\frac2{k-1}\right)
\frac{a_{m-3}}{a_{m-2}}\\
&+
z^2\left(4+\frac2{k-1}\right)
\frac{a_{m-4}}{a_{m-2}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every term after \((z+1)^2\) is nonnegative. Thus

\[
\theta_m(z)
\le
\left(\frac{z-1}{z+1}\right)^2
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
-\log\theta_m(z)
\ge
\beta(z)
:=
2\log\frac{z+1}{z-1}.
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
0\le
\frac{\partial_z\log\theta_m(z)}
{-\log\theta_m(z)}
\le
\frac{2}{(z-1)\beta(z)}.
}
\]
