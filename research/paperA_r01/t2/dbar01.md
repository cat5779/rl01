# Independent audit: the sharp noncommutative \(\bar d\) bound on sofic groups

**Verdict: PROVED in the countable sofic, single-orbit setting.** Let \(\Gamma\) be a
countable sofic group, let \(A,B\in R(\Gamma)\) be positive contractions on
\(\ell ^2(\Gamma)\), and let \(\mathbf P^A,\mathbf P^B\) be their determinantal
processes. With the canonical group trace

\[
 \tau(T)=\langle T\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle
\]

and Ornstein's invariant joining distance

\[
 \bar d(\mu,\nu)=
 \min\{\Pr[X_e\ne Y_e]:(X,Y)\text{ is a }\Gamma\text{-invariant joining of }\mu,\nu\},
\]

one has

\[
 \boxed{\bar d(\mathbf P^A,\mathbf P^B)\le \tau|A-B|.}
\]

No commutativity assumption on \(A\) and \(B\) is used. The constant \(1\) is
optimal. The proof below also establishes the finite-dimensional inequality

\[
 W_H(\mathbf P^A,\mathbf P^B)\le \operatorname{Tr}|A-B|
\]

for arbitrary finite Hermitian positive contractions, with the ordinary,
unnormalized matrix trace.

This audit checked the mathematical source titled **“行列式过程的不变耦合与最优迹范数界”
(“Invariant Couplings and the Optimal Trace-Norm Bound for Determinantal
Processes”)**, dated 27 September 2026. Only its polygonal argument and the
sofic ordered-coupling input are certified here. Its separate birth-process
construction for arbitrary countable groups was outside the assigned scope and
remains **INCOMPLETE / NOT REVIEWED BY THIS AUDIT**.

## 1. Exact source scope

The following statements were checked in the original published PDFs.

1. Lyons--Thom, *Invariant coupling of determinantal measures on sofic groups*,
   Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 36 (2016), 574--607, Theorem 2.1: if \(E\) is
   finite and \(Q_1\le Q_2\) are positive contractions on \(\ell^2(E)\), then
   \(\mathbf P^{Q_1}\preceq\mathbf P^{Q_2}\). By Strassen's theorem this gives a
   monotone coupling. No commutativity is assumed.
2. The same paper, Theorem 5.1 (printed p. 588): if \(\Gamma\) is sofic and
   \(S\) is a **finite generating set**, then
   \(0\le Q_1\le Q_2\le I\) in \(R(\Gamma)\), or in
   \(R(\Gamma,S)\), admit a \(\Gamma\)-invariant monotone coupling. Thus the
   theorem as printed directly covers finitely generated sofic groups.
3. Printed pp. 593--594 define \(\bar d\), prove its triangle inequality by a
   relatively independent joining, and state in Lemma 7.2 that an invariant
   monotone coupling attains the one-site density difference.
4. Immediately after Lemma 7.8 on printed p. 595, the authors note that the
   Schatten \(1\)-norm bound is known in the abelian case and for commuting
   kernels, and say that they do not know whether it always holds. Lemma 7.9
   then proves only
   \[
     \bar d(\mathbf P^Q,\mathbf P^{Q'})
       \le 6\,3^{2/3}\,\|Q-Q'\|_1^{1/3},
   \]
   together with strong-operator continuity. Printed p. 597 remarks that the
   sharp inequality appears numerically to hold even for finite matrices
   without invariance.
5. Lyons's ICM 2014 article, *Determinantal probability: basic properties and
   conjectures*, Theorem 5.6, restates the ordered invariant coupling in its
   sofic section. The unnumbered paragraph at the start of printed p. 158 asks
   about the sharp Schatten \(1\)-norm inequality. The following Conjecture 5.7
   is a different question about Fuglede--Kadison determinants and Bernoulli
   domination.
6. Lyons--Steif, *Stationary determinantal processes: phase multiplicity,
   Bernoullicity, entropy, and domination*, Duke Math. J. 120 (2003),
   Proposition 3.4, proves the commutative \(\mathbb Z^d\) predecessor
   \(\bar d(\mathbf P^f,\mathbf P^g)\le\int|f-g|\).

The countable-sofic extension used below is not attributed to the printed
statement of Lyons--Thom Theorem 5.1; it is proved here from that theorem.

## 2. Ordered couplings for countable sofic groups

### Proposition 2.1

Let \(\Gamma\) be a countable sofic group and let
\(0\le C\le D\le I\) lie in \(R(\Gamma)\). Then
\(\mathbf P^C\) and \(\mathbf P^D\) admit a \(\Gamma\)-invariant monotone
coupling. Consequently,

\[
 \bar d(\mathbf P^C,\mathbf P^D)=\tau(D-C).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Enumerate \(\Gamma\) and let
\(H_1\le H_2\le\cdots\) be the finitely generated subgroups generated by
successive initial segments, so that \(\bigcup_nH_n=\Gamma\). Subgroups of a
sofic group are sofic. Let \(P_n\) be the coordinate projection onto
\(\ell^2(H_n)\), and put

\[
 C^{(n)}=P_nC|_{\ell^2(H_n)},\qquad
 D^{(n)}=P_nD|_{\ell^2(H_n)}.
\]

These are \(H_n\)-equivariant positive contractions and
\(C^{(n)}\le D^{(n)}\). Lyons--Thom Theorem 5.1 therefore gives an
\(H_n\)-invariant monotone joining \(\kappa_n\) of their DPPs.

Copy \(\kappa_n\) independently to every left coset \(gH_n\), identifying
\(H_n\) with that coset by \(h\mapsto gh\). This law is independent of the
choice of the representative \(g\): replacing \(g\) by \(gh_0\) changes the
identification by left translation by \(h_0\in H_n\), and \(\kappa_n\) is
\(H_n\)-invariant. Left multiplication by any \(\gamma\in\Gamma\) permutes
the cosets and preserves each copied factor law. Hence the product joining
\(\widehat\kappa_n\) is \(\Gamma\)-invariant and monotone.

Its marginals are DPPs with block-diagonal kernels

\[
 C_n(x,y)=\begin{cases}C(x,y),&x^{-1}y\in H_n,\\0,&x^{-1}y\notin H_n,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 D_n(x,y)=\begin{cases}D(x,y),&x^{-1}y\in H_n,\\0,&x^{-1}y\notin H_n.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Indeed, these are direct sums of the corresponding compressed kernels over
the left cosets, and a DPP of a block-diagonal kernel is the independent
product of the block DPPs.

For every finite \(F\subset\Gamma\), eventually \(F^{-1}F\subset H_n\).
Then \(C_n[F]=C[F]\) and \(D_n[F]=D[F]\). Thus the two marginals of
\(\widehat\kappa_n\) converge weakly to \(\mathbf P^C\) and \(\mathbf P^D\),
respectively; in fact their finite DPP inclusion probabilities are eventually
equal to the target probabilities.

The space of probability measures on
\((\{0,1\}^{\Gamma})^2\) is compact. Take a weakly convergent subsequence.
Invariance is closed under weak convergence, and
\(\{(x,y):x\subseteq y\}\) is closed. The limit is therefore a
\(\Gamma\)-invariant monotone joining of the desired marginals.

In any monotone joining \(X\subseteq Y\),

\[
 \Pr[X_e\ne Y_e]=\mathbb E(Y_e-X_e)=\tau(D-C).
\]

Every joining, monotone or not, has mismatch probability at least the absolute
difference of the two one-site means. This proves (2.1). \(\square\)

### Why the coset step is necessary

Compressing to \(H_n\) alone produces only an \(H_n\)-joining. The independent
product over all **left** cosets produces a genuinely \(\Gamma\)-invariant
joining. Its marginal kernel deletes matrix entries between different cosets;
it does not claim that the original DPP factors over those cosets. The deleted
entries return exactly on every fixed finite window once \(F^{-1}F\subset H_n\).

## 3. Triangle inequality and invariant gluing

The minimum in the definition of \(\bar d\) exists: invariant joinings form a
nonempty closed subset of the compact space of probability measures on
\((\{0,1\}^{\Gamma})^2\), and the one-coordinate mismatch cost is continuous.

Suppose \(\pi_{12}\) joins \(\mu_1\) to \(\mu_2\) and \(\pi_{23}\) joins
\(\mu_2\) to \(\mu_3\), with both joinings invariant. Disintegrate over the
common middle coordinate and, conditionally on it, choose the two endpoint
coordinates independently. The resulting relative product is invariant. One
precise reason is that translating its two conditional kernels gives
disintegrations of the same invariant pair measures; uniqueness of
disintegration, for each element of the countable group, identifies the
translated relative product with the original one. Therefore

\[
 \mathbf 1_{\{X_e\ne Z_e\}}
 \le \mathbf 1_{\{X_e\ne Y_e\}}+
       \mathbf 1_{\{Y_e\ne Z_e\}}
\]

gives the triangle inequality. No amenability and no averaging over the group
is involved.

## 4. Manuscript-ready sharp theorem

### Theorem 4.1 (sharp trace-norm bound)

Let \(\Gamma\) be a countable sofic group. For any positive contractions
\(A,B\in R(\Gamma)\),

\[
 \bar d(\mathbf P^A,\mathbf P^B)\le\tau|A-B|.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The constant \(1\) is best possible.

### Proof

Set \(T=B-A\) and write its positive/negative-part decomposition as

\[
 T=T_+-T_-,\qquad T_+,T_-\ge0,\qquad T_++T_-=|T|.
\]

This is functional calculus for the single self-adjoint operator \(T\); it
does not require \(A\) and \(B\) to commute. Since \(-I\le T\le I\), one has
\(0\le T_+,T_-\le I\). They remain \(\Gamma\)-equivariant.

Fix \(N\ge1\) and define, for \(0\le k\le N\),

\[
 R_k=\frac{(N-k)A+kB}{N+1},
\]

and, for \(0\le k<N\),

\[
 S_k=R_k+\frac{T_+}{N+1}.
\]

All these operators are positive contractions. Indeed,

\[
 0\le R_k\le\frac{N}{N+1}I,
 \qquad
 0\le S_k\le\frac{N}{N+1}I+\frac1{N+1}I=I.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 S_k-R_k=\frac{T_+}{N+1},\qquad
 S_k-R_{k+1}=\frac{T_-}{N+1}.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus every edge of the polygonal chain

\[
 A,\ R_0,\ S_0,\ R_1,\ S_1,\ldots,
 R_{N-1},\ S_{N-1},\ R_N,\ B
\]

joins two ordered kernels. Proposition 2.1, followed by the invariant triangle
inequality, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \bar d(\mathbf P^A,\mathbf P^B)
 &\le \frac{\tau(A)}{N+1}
  +\frac{N\tau(T_++T_-)}{N+1}
  +\frac{\tau(B)}{N+1}\\
 &=\frac{\tau(A)+\tau(B)+N\tau|A-B|}{N+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Letting \(N\to\infty\) proves (4.1). This passage only takes the limit of
numerical upper bounds; it does not require continuity of \(\bar d\).

For sharpness take \(A=0\) and \(B=pI\), \(0<p\le1\). The first process is
identically empty, while the second has one-site density \(p\). Hence every
joining has mismatch probability \(p\), and
\(\tau|A-B|=p\). \(\square\)

### Trace normalization

The group trace above satisfies \(\tau(I)=1\). If \(\Gamma\) is finite of
cardinality \(n\), then

\[
 \tau(T)=\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}(T).
\]

This normalization is forced by the one-site definition of \(\bar d\). It
must not be confused with the unnormalized total Hamming cost in the next
section.

## 5. Arbitrary finite Hermitian kernels

### Theorem 5.1

Let \(E\) be finite, and let \(A,B\) be arbitrary Hermitian positive
contractions on \(\mathbb C^E\). If

\[
 W_H(\mu,\nu)=\min\mathbb E|X\mathbin\triangle Y|
\]

where the minimum runs over all couplings, then

\[
 \boxed{W_H(\mathbf P^A,\mathbf P^B)\le
        \operatorname{Tr}|A-B|.}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

If \(0\le C\le D\le I\), Lyons--Thom Theorem 2.1 and Strassen give a
monotone coupling. Its Hamming cost is

\[
 \mathbb E|Y\setminus X|
 =\mathbb E|Y|-\mathbb E|X|
 =\operatorname{Tr}(D-C).
\]

No coupling can have smaller cost, since
\(\mathbb E|X\triangle Y|\ge
|\mathbb E|Y|-\mathbb E|X||\). Hence the ordered cost is exactly
\(\operatorname{Tr}(D-C)\). Apply the polygon from the proof of Theorem 4.1,
replace \(\tau\) by \(\operatorname{Tr}\), and use the ordinary Wasserstein
triangle inequality. This proves (5.1), again without commutativity. \(\square\)

If \(E=\Gamma\) is a finite group of order \(n\) and the kernels are
equivariant, averaging any optimal transport plan over the finite group keeps
its total Hamming cost and makes it invariant. An invariant plan has equal
mismatch probability at every site, so

\[
 \bar d(\mathbf P^A,\mathbf P^B)=\frac1nW_H(\mathbf P^A,\mathbf P^B).
\]

This agrees exactly with \(\tau=\operatorname{Tr}/n\).

## 6. Why a one-step common upper bound fails

The polygonal scaling is essential. Consider

\[
 A=\frac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}16&12\\12&9\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B=\frac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}9&12\\12&16\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Both are rank-one orthogonal projections. Here

\[
 B-A=\frac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}-7&0\\0&7\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 (B-A)_+=\frac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&7\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore

\[
 A+(B-A)_+=\frac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}16&12\\12&16\end{pmatrix}
\]

has eigenvalues \(28/25\) and \(4/25\), so it is not a contraction. This
**DISPROVES** the tempting shortcut through the common upper operator
\(A+(B-A)_+\). It does not challenge the theorem: (4.2) reserves exactly the
\(1/(N+1)\) spectral headroom needed for each positive increment.

## 8. Second-pass review of the unified Theorem D and §7

**STATUS: CORRECT; SECOND-PASS FLAGS CLOSED.** The unified
statement of Theorem D and its complete §7 proof were reviewed independently
after they were assembled. No mathematical premise was lost in the
integration.

The following interfaces were checked again.

1. The theorem is restricted to the regular action of a **countable sofic**
   group and kernels in \(R(\Gamma)\). It does not accidentally claim the
   finite-type or arbitrary-action extension.
2. The manuscript states accurately that published Lyons--Thom Theorem 5.1
   assumes a finite generating set. It labels the passage to countable sofic
   groups as an argument supplied in the manuscript, rather than as part of
   the published theorem.
3. The subgroup compression is ordered and equivariant; the independent
   joining over left cosets is representative-independent and
   \(\Gamma\)-invariant; the displayed block kernels are correct; and their
   finite principal matrices eventually equal the target principal matrices.
   Compactness therefore yields the claimed ordered joining.
4. The ordered cost is the canonical normalized trace difference. The
   relatively independent joining remains invariant, so the triangle
   inequality is valid without amenability.
5. Every polygon vertex is a positive contraction, every consecutive pair is
   ordered in one direction, the two endpoint costs are included, and the
   numerical limit proves the same fixed-endpoint inequality without invoking
   continuity of \(\bar d\).
6. The finite-dimensional corollary correctly replaces the normalized group
   trace by the ordinary unnormalized trace and uses total Hamming cost. The
   finite-group identity \(\bar d=W_H/|\Gamma|\) follows by averaging an
   optimal coupling.
7. The source locations are correct: the sharp question follows Lemma 7.8 on
   printed p. 595; Lemma 7.9 has the \(1/3\)-power bound; and the commutative
   predecessor is Lyons--Steif Proposition 3.4.

The two small points identified in the first second-pass reading have now been
closed in the current draft:

- The sharpness sentence now states \(0<p\le1\), so \(B=pI\) is explicitly a
  positive contraction.
- The finite \(W_H\) corollary now cites Lyons--Thom Theorem 2.1 at its use of
  finite DPP order.

The countable-sofic paragraph also now says explicitly that the compressions
are principal compressions and explains directly, from the finite-set
definition, why each finitely generated subgroup \(H_n\) is sofic. These
clarifications agree with the proof audited above.

The current unified Theorem D and §7 therefore deserve the unqualified verdict
**PROVED** within their stated countable-sofic scope.
