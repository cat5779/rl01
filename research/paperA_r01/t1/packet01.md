# Specialist reading brief

The [English draft](paperA.md) is ready for a probability or ergodic-theory specialist's mathematical reading. The [source register](sources01.md) separates published inputs from existing candidate arguments. This brief has not been sent to an external person; the attached reviews are independent AI pre-reviews.

## First reading: Question 7.7

Read Theorem A, §2, §3 and Appendix A.1. The claim uses one iid uniform per element of the original countable \(\Gamma\)-set \(W\), in the generalized Bernoulli convention of Lyons–Thom. It asserts a total Borel map with pointwise equivariance. For the random-graph consequence, §4 adds joint Borel dependence and naturality under all isomorphisms; a fixed-graph factor theorem alone would not suffice.

The most useful objections would identify a missing measurable dependence, an unjustified passage from finite to full observation histories, or a filtration problem in the innovations step. In particular, check whether the distinction between a pathwise deterministic construction and its probabilistic law identification is clear without consulting earlier project files.

## Second reading: the pointwise-optimality counterexample

Read Theorem C and §§6.2–6.3 first; Appendix B is the finite-support strengthening. The counterexample uses the WUSF gradient projection on the three-regular Bass–Serre tree of \(C_3*C_3\), whose edges form one free transitive group orbit. Its lower Bernoulli parameter is \(1/2\), while its FK determinant is zero. The proof then removes spectral endpoints and finally truncates the inverse Laplacian to an explicit finite-support positive contraction.

Check the action and orientation, the conditional-probability lower bound, the determinant formula for connected edge sets, and the exact positive-contraction and determinant margins after truncation. BLPS §11 is explicitly credited for the classical tree lower coupling. The claimed consequence is failure of universal *pointwise* optimality; a classification of nonamenable groups and kernels is not claimed.

## Third reading: interfaces and readability

Check whether a reader can distinguish:

- ordinary stochastic domination from an invariant monotone joining;
- an arbitrary \(W\)-indexed product from a regular \(\Gamma\)-indexed source;
- one regular orbit from finite-type matrix kernels and their unnormalized trace;
- an everywhere-defined factor from a finitary factor;
- the original factor Question 7.7 from the separate trace-norm question after Lemma 7.8.

Section 7 is independent of the new sampling argument. It uses Lyons–Thom's sofic invariant ordered-coupling theorem and a polygon that stays inside the contraction interval. Its countable-group extension is written out before the polygon. Li–Thom Theorem 1.4 is used only for the amenable determinant approximation; its positivity, amenability and trace premises are listed in §6.1 and the source register.

An external report should distinguish a false statement with a counterexample from an incomplete proof step, and should identify any exact prior result that subsumes a claimed conclusion. No novelty or priority assertion is requested or assumed.
