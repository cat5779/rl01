PROVED

For the fixed \(\varepsilon\), use the **same selector for every support bound**:
\[
\boxed{
F_{K,P}
=\operatorname*{argmin}_{f\in\mathcal F_E(K,P)}
\frac12\sum_{S,i\notin S}f(S,i)^2.
}
\tag{1}
\]
The support bound will enter only the estimate, not this definition.

Here is an explicit constant. Set
\[
m_*=2s,\qquad N_*=m_*2^{m_*-1},
\]
\[
H_s=(m_*N_*)^{N_*/2},
\qquad
\Lambda_s=\sqrt{N_*}\,H_s,
\qquad
d_\varepsilon
=1+\left(\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{2\varepsilon}\right)^2.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
C_{\varepsilon,s}
=2+\frac{12\Lambda_s d_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}
}
\tag{2}
\]
satisfies the theorem. In particular, it is finite for every fixed \((\varepsilon,s)\) and independent of \(|E|\).

All vector norms below are unnormalized; matrix norms with subscript \(1\) are trace norms. The proof establishes existence of (1), its exact conditioning compatibility, the uniform active-set bound, and all required regularity properties.

## 1. Nonempty fibers, normalization, and inactive coordinates

For a signed upward edge flow \(f\) and any function \(\phi\) on configurations,
\[
\sum_S\phi(S)\operatorname{div}f(S)
=
\sum_{S,i\notin S}f(S,i)
\bigl(\phi(S\cup\{i\})-\phi(S)\bigr).
\]
Consequently, if \(\operatorname{div}f=b_{K,P}\), differentiation of the DPP identities
\[
\mathbb E_K|X|=\operatorname{tr}K,
\qquad
\mathbb P_K(j\in X)=K_{jj}
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{S,i\notin S}f(S,i)=1,
\qquad
\sum_{S:j\notin S}f(S,j)=P_{jj}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus normalization is redundant once the divergence is imposed. Moreover, for a nonnegative flow, \(P_{jj}=0\) forces every edge in direction \(j\) to vanish.

For completeness, nonemptiness holds without any support restriction. We use the nested-projection coupling theorem: projection DPPs corresponding to nested subspaces of codimension one admit a monotone coupling that adds exactly one point. This is the codimension-one case of Lyons, Proposition 10.3.

Put \(h=\varepsilon/2\) and \(B=K+hP\). On
\[
\mathcal H=\mathbb C^E\oplus\operatorname{ran}P\oplus\mathbb C^E
\]
define the isometry
\[
Vx=\bigl(K^{1/2}x,\sqrt h\,Px,(I-B)^{1/2}x\bigr).
\]
Indeed, \(V^*V=K+hP+I-B=I\). Let \(\Pi_0,\Pi_1\) project onto the first summand and the first two summands, respectively. Then
\[
V^*\Pi_0V=K,\qquad V^*\Pi_1V=B,
\]
and their ranges are nested with dimension difference one.

Complete the orthonormal vectors \(Ve_i\) to a coordinate basis of \(\mathcal H\). Apply the projection coupling in this basis and restrict both configurations to the coordinates \(Ve_i\). The resulting coupling \((X,Y)\) has DPP kernels \(K,B\) and satisfies
\[
X\subseteq Y,\qquad |Y\setminus X|\le1.
\]
Define
\[
f(S,i)=h^{-1}\mathbb P(X=S,\ Y=S\cup\{i\}).
\]
Exact probabilities are affine along a rank-one kernel line, so
\[
\operatorname{div}f=(p_B-p_K)/h=b_{K,P}.
\]
Also,
\[
f_{\rm out}(S)\le h^{-1}p_K(S)=\frac2\varepsilon p_K(S),
\]
and
\[
\sum_{S,i\notin S}f(S,i)
=h^{-1}\mathbb E(|Y|-|X|)
=h^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(B-K)=1.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal F_E(K,P)\) is nonempty. It is a closed subset of the unit simplex of edge flows, hence compact. Strict convexity makes (1) well-defined and unique.

We will also use
\[
\boxed{\|p_A-p_B\|_1\le2\|A-B\|_1}
\tag{4}
\]
for positive contractions \(A,B\). One can obtain it directly from the preceding existence argument. At any interior kernel \(K\), a nonnegative unit flow for a rank-one projector \(R\) gives
\[
\|b_{K,R}\|_1\le2.
\]
Spectrally decompose a Hermitian direction \(H=\sum_j\lambda_jR_j\). Linearity of differentiation gives
\[
\|Dp_K[H]\|_1\le2\sum_j|\lambda_j|=2\|H\|_1.
\]
Integration along an interior segment proves (4) there. Boundary kernels follow by replacing \(A,B\) with
\[
(1-2t)A+tI,\qquad (1-2t)B+tI
\]
and letting \(t\downarrow0\). Continuity and rank-one affinity follow, for example, from inclusion-exclusion:
\[
p_K(S)
=(-1)^{|E\setminus S|}
\det(K-\Pi_{E\setminus S}),
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\Pi_U\) is the diagonal projection onto \(U\).

## 2. A global least-norm Lipschitz lemma for fixed inequalities

Let \(\mathsf A\) be a fixed real matrix with \(d\) columns. For each feasible right-hand side \(r\), define
\[
Q(r)=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\mathsf A x\le r\},
\qquad
u(r)=\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in Q(r)}\frac12\|x\|_2^2.
\]
For every nonempty linearly independent set \(I\) of rows, let \(\mathsf A_I\) be the corresponding submatrix, and put
\[
H(\mathsf A)=
\max_I \sigma_{\min}(\mathsf A_I)^{-1}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\|u(r)-u(r')\|_2
\le H(\mathsf A)\|r-r'\|_2
}
\tag{6}
\]
for all feasible \(r,r'\). If all rows vanish, \(u\equiv0\).

Here is a proof including degenerate active sets. At \(y=u(r)\), first-order optimality implies that \(-y\) belongs to the cone generated by the active row normals. To see this, any vector \(z\) satisfying
\[
\mathsf A_i z\le0
\quad\text{for all active rows }i
\]
is a feasible direction for sufficiently small positive steps, and hence
\(\langle y,z\rangle\ge0\). Separation from the cone of active normals gives the stated conclusion.

Choose a conic representation of \(-y\) using the fewest positive coefficients. Its row normals are linearly independent: a linear dependence would permit adjustment of the coefficients, preserving nonnegativity, until one positive coefficient became zero. Unless \(y=0\), therefore,
\[
-y=\mathsf A_I^*\lambda,\qquad
\lambda\ge0,\qquad
\mathsf A_Iy=r_I
\]
for an independent set \(I\). Solving,
\[
y=L_Ir_I,
\qquad
L_I=\mathsf A_I^*(\mathsf A_I\mathsf A_I^*)^{-1},
\qquad
\|L_I\|_{\rm op}=\sigma_{\min}(\mathsf A_I)^{-1}.
\tag{7}
\]

Conversely, this formula is the unique minimizer whenever
\[
\mathsf A L_Ir_I\le r,
\qquad
-(\mathsf A_I\mathsf A_I^*)^{-1}r_I\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, the associated multiplier gives
\[
\langle y,z-y\rangle
=-\lambda^*\mathsf A_I(z-y)\ge0
\]
for every feasible \(z\), which proves optimality.

Conditions (8) define a closed convex polyhedral region of right-hand sides. Include also the region \(r\ge0\), where \(u(r)=0\). These finitely many regions cover the feasible right-hand sides, and their formulas agree on overlaps by uniqueness.

The segment joining feasible \(r,r'\) is feasible, by interpolation of feasible witnesses. Its intersection with each region is an interval. Subdivide at all interval endpoints. On each subsegment one formula (7), or the zero formula, applies. Summing its Lipschitz estimates along the segment proves (6).

Thus this lemma supplies a globally consistent selector, rather than merely a Hausdorff estimate.

## 3. Uniform active-set constants on all cubes of size at most \(2s\)

On an \(m\)-coordinate cube, write
\[
N_m=m2^{m-1}
\]
for the number of edges, \(\mathsf D_m\) for the divergence matrix, and \(\mathsf O_m\) for the outgoing-sum matrix.

By (3), the original flow fiber is exactly
\[
\mathsf A_m f\le r(M,R),
\]
where
\[
\mathsf A_m=
\begin{bmatrix}
-I\\
\mathsf D_m\\
-\mathsf D_m\\
\mathsf O_m
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
r(M,R)=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
b_{M,R}\\
-b_{M,R}\\
(2/\varepsilon)p_M
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{9}
\]
There is no omitted normalization condition: it follows from the two divergence inequalities.

Every entry of \(\mathsf A_m\) is an integer. Every row has squared Euclidean norm at most \(m\): a divergence row has exactly \(m\) entries of absolute value one, an outgoing row has at most \(m\), and a row of \(-I\) has one.

Let \(I\) contain \(k\) independent rows, where
\[
1\le k\le N_m\le N_*
\qquad(m\le m_*).
\]
Its Gram matrix
\[
G=\mathsf A_{m,I}\mathsf A_{m,I}^*
\]
is positive definite and integer-valued. Consequently,
\[
\det G\ge1,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}G\le km\le m_*N_*.
\]
Every eigenvalue is at most \(m_*N_*\). Taking the product of the eigenvalues yields
\[
\lambda_{\min}(G)
\ge(m_*N_*)^{-(k-1)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H(\mathsf A_m)
\le(m_*N_*)^{(N_*-1)/2}
\le H_s
\qquad(1\le m\le2s).
}
\tag{10}
\]
This proves the required finite uniform active-set bound explicitly. It depends only on \(s\), not on the kernel, direction, probabilities, or ambient coordinate set.

Next let \(M,N\) be \(\varepsilon\)-gapped kernels on the same \(m\le2s\) coordinates, and let \(R,Q\) be rank-one projectors. Set
\[
\delta_M=\|M-N\|_1,\qquad
\delta_R=\|R-Q\|_1.
\]
Both \(M+\varepsilon R\) and \(N+\varepsilon Q\) are positive contractions. Rank-one affinity and (4) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|b_{M,R}-b_{N,Q}\|_1
&=\varepsilon^{-1}
\|(p_{M+\varepsilon R}-p_M)
 -(p_{N+\varepsilon Q}-p_N)\|_1\\
&\le\frac4\varepsilon\delta_M+2\delta_R.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Also,
\[
\left\|\frac2\varepsilon(p_M-p_N)\right\|_1
\le\frac4\varepsilon\delta_M.
\]
For the right-hand sides in (9), it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|r(M,R)-r(N,Q)\|_2
&\le2\|b_{M,R}-b_{N,Q}\|_1
  +\frac2\varepsilon\|p_M-p_N\|_1\\
&\le\frac{12}\varepsilon\delta_M+4\delta_R.
\end{aligned}
\]
Apply (6), (10), and \(\|x\|_1\le\sqrt{N_m}\|x\|_2\). For the selector (1) on these coordinate sets,
\[
\boxed{
\|F_{M,R}-F_{N,Q}\|_1
\le
\Lambda_s\left(
\frac{12}\varepsilon\|M-N\|_1
+4\|R-Q\|_1
\right),
\qquad m\le2s.
}
\tag{12}
\]
This estimate includes zero coordinates and all support degeneracies within the local coordinate set.

## 4. Conditioning: exact identities and dimension-free stability

Let \(J\subseteq E\) contain \(\operatorname{supp}P\), put \(O=E\setminus J\), and fix \(T\subseteq O\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_T(K)&=K_O-\Pi_{O\setminus T},\\
M_T(K)&=K_J-K_{JO}B_T(K)^{-1}K_{OJ},\\
q_K(T)&=p_{K_O}(T).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
For empty \(O\), the correction is zero and \(q_K(\varnothing)=1\).

The inverse is uniformly controlled. Indeed,
\[
B_T(K)
=(K_O-\tfrac12I)+(\tfrac12I-\Pi_{O\setminus T}).
\]
The second summand has every singular value equal to \(1/2\), while the first has operator norm at most \(1/2-\varepsilon\). Hence
\[
\boxed{\|B_T(K)^{-1}\|_{\rm op}\le\varepsilon^{-1}.}
\tag{14}
\]
By (5), \(q_K(T)\) is a signed determinant of this invertible matrix. Since it is a probability, it follows that \(q_K(T)>0\).

The conditioning identities are
\[
p_K(T\cup A)=q_K(T)p_{M_T(K)}(A),
\qquad A\subseteq J,
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
\varepsilon I_J\preceq M_T(K)\preceq(1-\varepsilon)I_J.
\tag{16}
\]
The factorization follows from (5) and the block determinant formula.

To verify gap preservation, pin coordinates successively. For a gapped block matrix
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}a&c^*\\c&D\end{pmatrix},
\]
the occupied and absent conditional kernels are
\[
D-\frac{cc^*}{a},
\qquad
D+\frac{cc^*}{1-a}.
\]
For the first,
\[
x^*(D-cc^*/a)x
=\min_{z\in\mathbb C}(z,x)^*H(z,x)
\ge\varepsilon\|x\|^2,
\]
while its upper bound follows from \(D-cc^*/a\preceq D\). The second is at least \(D\succeq\varepsilon I\); applying the preceding lower-bound argument to \(I-H\) proves its upper bound. Successive Schur-complement identities identify the resulting kernel with (13), proving (16).

The conditional kernels satisfy the pointwise estimate
\[
\boxed{
\|M_T(K)-M_T(L)\|_1
\le d_\varepsilon\|K-L\|_1.
}
\tag{17}
\]
To prove it, put \(K_t=K+t(L-K)\) and
\[
W_t=[\,I_J,\,-K_{t,JO}B_T(K_t)^{-1}\,].
\]
Differentiating the Schur complement gives
\[
\frac d{dt}M_T(K_t)=W_t(L-K)W_t^*.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\|K_{t,JO}\|_{\rm op}
\le\|K_t-\tfrac12I\|_{\rm op}
\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon,
\]
so (14) implies
\[
\|W_t\|_{\rm op}^2
\le1+\left(\frac{1/2-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)^2
=d_\varepsilon.
\]
Trace-norm multiplication and integration prove (17). In particular,
\[
\sum_Tq_K(T)\|M_T(K)-M_T(L)\|_1
\le d_\varepsilon\|K-L\|_1.
\tag{18}
\]
Also, (4) and trace-norm compression give
\[
\|q_K-q_L\|_1\le2\|K-L\|_1.
\tag{19}
\]

Finally, \(P\) has zero rows and columns outside \(J\). Its perturbation leaves \(q_K(T)\), the cross blocks, and \(B_T(K)\) unchanged. Therefore
\[
M_T(K+hP)=M_T(K)+hP_J,
\]
and differentiation of (15) gives
\[
\boxed{
b_{K,P}(T\cup A)
=q_K(T)b_{M_T(K),P_J}(A).
}
\tag{20}
\]

## 5. Exact product compatibility of the one global selector

We now prove that for **every** \(J\supseteq\operatorname{supp}P\), selector (1) obeys
\[
\boxed{
F_{K,P}(T\cup A,i)
=q_K(T)F_{M_T(K),P_J}(A,i),
\qquad i\in J\setminus A,
}
\tag{21}
\]
and vanishes on directions outside \(J\).

By (3), every feasible nonnegative flow vanishes on directions outside \(J\). All remaining edges split into disjoint blocks indexed by the outside pattern \(T\).

By (15) and (20), the nonnegativity, divergence, and capacity constraints in block \(T\) are exactly those of
\[
q_K(T)\,\mathcal F_J(M_T(K),P_J).
\]
The mass of that block is forced to be \(q_K(T)\): multiply its divergence by \(|A|\) and use the local identity (3). Thus the total-mass condition does not introduce any coupling between different blocks.

Conversely, any choice from each of these scaled local fibers gives a full feasible flow. Under the natural block embedding,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_E(K,P)
=
\prod_{T\subseteq O}
q_K(T)\,\mathcal F_J(M_T(K),P_J),
}
\tag{22}
\]
with zero edges in the remaining directions.

Writing a block as \(q_K(T)g_T\), the objective in (1) becomes
\[
\frac12\sum_Tq_K(T)^2\|g_T\|_2^2.
\]
All weights are positive, and the feasible set is a Cartesian product. Each block therefore minimizes independently; multiplication by \(q_K(T)^2\) does not change its unique minimizer. This proves (21).

Only positivity of the conditioning probabilities is used here. No quantitative estimate involves their reciprocals.

In particular, (21) is valid for a minimal support set and for every larger auxiliary set. If \(P=E_i\), taking \(J=\{i\}\) gives
\[
F_{K,E_i}(S,i)=p_{K_{E\setminus\{i\}}}(S),
\qquad
F_{K,E_i}(S,j)=0\quad(j\ne i),
\tag{23}
\]
because the one-site derivative flow has its sole edge equal to one. Thus the coordinate selector is recovered exactly, independently of any auxiliary coordinate set. The same product identity gives compatibility on every intermediate support stratum.

## 6. Global estimate, degeneration, and covariance

Take any allowed pairs \((K,P),(L,Q)\) on the same finite \(E\), and set
\[
J=\operatorname{supp}P\cup\operatorname{supp}Q.
\]
Because both support sizes are at most \(s\),
\[
1\le |J|\le2s.
\]
This \(J\) is used only in the estimate; the selectors have already been fixed by (1).

Use (21) to express both selectors in these common edge blocks. Each local selected flow is nonnegative of mass one. Splitting the products therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|F_{K,P}-F_{L,Q}\|_1
&\le \|q_K-q_L\|_1\\
&\quad+\sum_Tq_K(T)
\|F_{M_T(K),P_J}-F_{M_T(L),Q_J}\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Apply the local estimate (12), the conditional estimate (18), and (19). Since \(P-Q\) is supported on \(J\), compression to \(J\) leaves its trace norm unchanged. We obtain
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\|F_{K,P}-F_{L,Q}\|_1
&\le
\left(2+\frac{12\Lambda_s d_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)
\|K-L\|_1\\
&\quad+4\Lambda_s\|P-Q\|_1.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{25}
\]
The outside-pattern sum is a probability average, not a count of patterns.

Because \(d_\varepsilon\ge1\) and \(\varepsilon<1/2\), the constant in (2) dominates \(4\Lambda_s\). Equation (25) proves (DFS).

This argument covers every intersection pattern of the supports, including disjoint supports. It also covers zero-coordinate degeneration and simultaneous changes of \(K,P\). In particular, whenever admissible \((K_r,P_r)\to(K,P)\) within the support-at-most-\(s\) domain, (25) gives
\[
F_{K_r,P_r}\longrightarrow F_{K,P},
\]
even when the support drops at the limit.

For each finite \(E\), the resulting map is Lipschitz on its entire allowed domain, hence Borel. No representative vector of \(P\) is selected, so all dependence is through the projector itself.

Let \(\sigma\) be a coordinate permutation, represented by \(U_\sigma\). Relabeling maps the original fiber bijectively onto the corresponding permuted fiber and preserves the squared Euclidean edge norm. Uniqueness in (1) consequently gives
\[
F_{U_\sigma K U_\sigma^*,\,U_\sigma P U_\sigma^*}
(\sigma S,\sigma i)
=
F_{K,P}(S,i).
\]
This proves coordinate-permutation equivariance.

Finally, membership in the original fiber gives nonnegativity, exact divergence, total mass one, and the specified outgoing capacity. The explicit constant (2) depends only on \(\varepsilon,s\), while the selector (1) is the same for every support bound.

Thus the theorem holds for **every fixed finite \(s\)**. No bound uniform as \(s\to\infty\), and hence no unrestricted arbitrary-support dimension-free theorem, is asserted. \(\square\)
