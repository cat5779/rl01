# SOL_PROTOCOL — selective review for the next round

This protocol was requested by the user for the next round of **four Web Pro researchers plus a limited Sol review budget**.

Do not assign all Sol reviewers in advance. The point is to spend review capacity only on results that can change the next research decision.

## Sol 1 — first decisive candidate

Use on the first Pro result that does at least one of the following:

- proves a true-law \(O(n)\) or \(o(n)\) signed payment that was previously open;
- creates a nonzero-width new \(a\) or \(c\) region outside the accepted baseline;
- proves an ordinary-density extension that is not a formal symmetry of the half-density case;
- closes the retained finite-dyadic signed sum;
- gives a genuine high-contrast theorem whose range accumulates at \(c=1\).

Audit the load-bearing chain from definitions. Re-run exact certificates if the theorem depends on them. Do not spend most of the review re-checking inherited accepted lemmas.

## Sol 2 — second independent load-bearing result

Use only after Sol 1 unless a second result is obviously independent and urgent.

Priority questions:

- Are moving-law derivatives and all probability-acceleration terms retained?
- Is a one-sided \(o(n)\) estimate being silently upgraded to an absolute-value estimate?
- Was a projection identity applied to a finite Toeplitz compression without leakage?
- Did a midpoint statement become a fixed-width interval without a uniform modulus?
- Did a fixed-density theorem get described as arbitrary density?
- Does the parameter range actually overlap the theorem it is supposed to combine with?

A useful Sol output is allowed to repair a local proof gap, but must label reviewer-added arguments separately from the Pro author's proof.

## Sol 3 — composition and coverage audit

Reserve for when two or more reviewed outputs are intended to prove a larger part of the ultimate theorem.

Build an explicit coverage table in \((\rho,c,a)\). Check:

1. the laws being compared are exactly the same law where combination is claimed;
2. the normalizations and parameter directions match;
3. limit order and value/derivative bridges are valid;
4. endpoint closure follows by an actual theorem/continuity argument;
5. unions of proved regions are not replaced by an unjustified convex hull;
6. no corrected-law sign is silently promoted to a true-law sign.

The final Sol should say exactly which subset of
\[
0<\rho<1,\quad0<c<1,\quad0\le a\le1-c
\]
is proved after composition and identify the smallest remaining open set.

## Review verdict format

For each load-bearing claim use one of:

- \`VERIFIED\`
- \`VERIFIED_SCOPED\`
- \`DISPROVED\`
- \`GAP\`

Then give the shortest exact reason that determines the verdict. Avoid broad approval language when only a component has been checked.
