# Sources and attribution boundaries

1. Russell Lyons, *Determinantal probability measures*, Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS **98** (2003), 167–212, [original article](https://numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0016-0.pdf), especially Theorem 8.1 and Proposition 10.3. Nested projection DPPs admit a monotone coupling whose difference has the prescribed codimension. A support-minimal dilation of the effects A, ww*, and I−A−ww* reduces a positive-contraction rank-one increment to this result; restriction to the original coordinates yields the at-most-one-point lemma. That lemma is not claimed as a new theorem here.

2. Russell Lyons and Andreas Thom, *Invariant Coupling of Determinantal Measures on Sofic Groups*, [author manuscript](https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/pdf/couple-pub.pdf). Their invariant coupling theorem and Bernoulli-factor question concern distinct conclusions. An invariant joining, a joint iid factor, and a factor conditional on a prescribed initial sample must not be interchanged.

3. The finite-support relative-transition statement supplied to the present problem is archived in PR #94, restricted to its complete reviewed core. It is a stated input to the conditional exhaustion theorem. The missing continuation of that source is outside the evidence used here.

The measurable-selection and parameterized-disintegration arguments are standard measure-theoretic inputs. The positive-flow construction must explicitly obtain all translated kernels from one chosen root disintegration to ensure covariance on every input.

This is a targeted attribution check. Priority for the assembled positive-flow observation, a comprehensive literature search, independent domain review, and formal verification are not certified.
